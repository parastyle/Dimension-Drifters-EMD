## Build
1. **A per-weapon capability**: does this weapon have a light, a laser, both, or neither? Derive it
   from weapon data rather than hand-listing 360 weapons. The B63 guns declare it; older weapons
   mostly have neither. Make the default honest — absent unless the weapon actually has one.
2. **A toggle**, on a sensible free key. Check the existing bindings first
   (`ArenaScene` registers `W,A,S,D,R,P,Q,E,Z,X,F,G,H,T,B,C,M,TAB,ESC,SPACE,SHIFT,CTRL,ONE..FIVE,
   arrows,ENTER`) and pick something unused. State your choice and why. If laser and light are
   separate, decide whether it is one cycling key or two — make the call and justify it.
3. **The render.** A laser is a thin bright line from the muzzle toward the aim point. A flashlight is
   a soft cone. Both originate at the WEAPON MUZZLE and follow the existing aim vector.
4. **Persist the toggle** across weapon swaps and across a run in the sensible way — a player who
   turned their light on does not want it off every time they switch guns. Use your judgement and say
   what you chose.

## STANDING LAWS — breaking these fails the batch
- **NO PLAYER AURAS.** Owner ruling, absolute: weapon VFX never renders a glow or aura around the
  player. A flashlight cone is a directional beam from the muzzle — it must NEVER become a radial
  glow centred on the character. This law has been violated before and the owner said "never again".
- **Never interrupt gameplay with a modal.** The toggle is instant, no menu.
- The laser/light must not wash out gameplay semaphores (telegraphs, parry windows, the gas ring).
  Keep it readable but subordinate — it is flavour plus a little utility, not a lighting overhaul.
- Multiplayer: other players must see your light. Decide whether the toggle is server state or a
  client cosmetic broadcast, and justify it. Do NOT add per-frame network traffic for this.
