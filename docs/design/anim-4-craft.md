# Animation craft: targets and transition technique

## Mandate and working constraints

This report diagnoses what good combo transitions should look like and which animation technique best fits Dimension Drifters' Phaser part rig. It will preserve the 14 shipped katana movesets, their beats, timing, hit windows, paths, and server authority; distinguish desirable impact snaps from accidental pose teleports; compare inertialization, cross-fades, motion matching, and authored linking poses using named sources; verify the actual seams in `SpriteRig.ts`, `melee.ts`, and `pose-language.ts`; and recommend an incremental option without changing product code. The only repository file I will write is this report. Findings will explicitly distinguish measured code facts from inference and craft judgment.

## Investigation log

- **Recorded before investigation:** The owner's floor is continuous parts, but the craft target also requires intentional velocity through combo links: anticipation into acceleration, a readable strike, follow-through, and either a moving hold into the next step or an authored settle. Sharp impact, parry, and hit-reaction changes may be desirable; accidental discontinuities at step boundaries, idle returns, chain expiry, equipment transitions, and interruptions are the candidate defects to verify.
- **Reporting rule verified:** `docs/sol-reports/README.md` requires a report-of-record created at the start, updated as work completes, with validation appended last. This task explicitly assigns the report-of-record to `docs/design/anim-4-craft.md`, so this file follows that append-as-you-go regime.

## 1am summary

Do not make every attack buttery. The game already has an 80 ms pose bridge; it misses new parent-root/shadow channels and, even where it works, it preserves position but throws away speed. Fix that coverage hole first, then make each approved combo pair's recovery flow into the next pair's anticipation using a small authored `carry / settle / snap` vocabulary. A continued combo should look like one thought: impact is crisp, the blade and body follow through, and that remaining energy becomes the next wind-up without returning to dead neutral. A dropped combo should breathe into the existing ready stance. Parry, impact, recoil, roll/tumble takeover, downed/death, render-depth flips, and true weapon identity changes must stay sharp. Preserve every attack sequence, clock, path, hit window, and server decision.

- **Measured high-visibility case:** the `backflip` primitive applies a full-turn `paperRotation` until `followEnd` (`packages/shared/src/melee.ts:400-421`), and `c072111` put that primitive into multiple shipped recipes (for example the Petalfall step at `melee.ts:1250-1270` and generated Riftstep choreography at `packages/shared/src/weapons-expansion.generated.ts:14808-14818`).
- **Inference, not a live capture:** if a new accepted step arrives while the previous backflip still has nonzero `paperRotation`, frame F displays the rotated parent. `triggerSwing` captures only child-local transforms. On frame F+1, `animate` resets the parent root, samples the new step near `t=0` (identity), and finally bridges only child locals. The entire child group therefore changes screen-space orientation on that exact accepted-beat frame even though the child-local unit test passes. The shadow can cut on the same frame. This is a concrete path for the reported “teleport.”
- **Craft diagnosis from measured math:** captured channels are position-continuous, but the bridge uses a fresh smoothstep whose slope is zero at elapsed 0 and at its deadline (`SpriteRig.ts:710-742`). It preserves the prior pose value, not the prior pose velocity. Thus it can turn a fast outgoing cut into an 80 ms decelerate/re-accelerate bridge: technically continuous, visibly “sliding cardboard.” The tests assert boundary value and end target but do not assert first derivative/velocity (`SpriteRig.combo-continuity.test.ts:125-136`).
- **Fair causality verdict:** `c072111` is not the origin of the general seam machinery, and its authored primitives correctly return to identity at `t=1`. It likely **exposed or worsened** the issue by adding much larger full-body trajectories plus an unbridged root/shadow channel. The strongest specific regression case is new root rotation crossing the old bridge's ownership boundary; the broader dead-stop quality issue predates the commit.

## Technique research

### Inertialization — the best runtime model, but not the first fix I would ship

David Bollo's GDC 2018 talk, [“Inertialization: High-Performance Animation Transitions in *Gears of War*”](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025165/Inertialization), describes replacing ordinary source/target cross-fades with a post-process. At the switch, the system measures the difference between what was on screen and the newly selected target, including velocity, then evaluates only the new animation while a residual correction decays to zero. Bollo's [SIGGRAPH 2017 supplemental math](https://cdn.gearsofwar.com/thecoalition/publications/SIGGRAPH%202017%20-%20High%20Performance%20Animation%20in%20Gears%20ofWar%204%20-%20Supplemental.pdf) is precise: a quintic curve matches pose difference and velocity at transition start and converges to zero at the end, independently for each scalar degree of freedom. That is why it preserves “where it was going,” not just “where it was.” It also avoids evaluating two complete animation graphs; Bollo's GDC overview calls out the near-doubling of evaluation cost during conventional blends.

Epic's current [Animation Blueprint blend-node documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/animation-blueprint-blend-nodes-in-unreal-engine) corroborates the production behavior: inertialization tracks pose motion, lets that motion continue toward the target, stops evaluating the source pose once the blend begins, works best with short transitions begun while the source is still moving, and permits selected curves to be filtered so they change instantly.

**Fit to this rig (my judgment): technically strong.** Bollo's core operates per scalar/vector degree of freedom, so it does not require bones. A Phaser part's local `x`, `y`, unwrapped rotation, and scale are exactly those kinds of channels. The rig could retain the last two final-rendered samples, estimate velocity, sample only the new authored step, and add a short decaying residual. Rotation needs shortest-arc/unwrapped handling; signed root scale, render-depth flips, visibility, weapon identity, and gameplay state should be filtered rather than inertialized. Crucially, the residual must be applied in one consistent local space after ordinary pose composition but before gear followers, and never to authoritative world/root position.

**Why not make full inertialization the first answer here (my judgment):** this code already has a cheap snapshot bridge, only eight katana primitives, explicit pair order, and known accepted timing. The immediate defect is incomplete channel ownership, and the craft problem is pair-specific motion design. Adding velocity history and interruption-safe residuals across a 10,713-line multi-writer rig before fixing those facts would be a larger surface than necessary. Inertialization is an excellent later safety net for remote cadence and unanticipated interrupts, not a substitute for authoring good exits and entries.

### Cross-fades and blend trees — traditional, useful, and dangerous when the blend consumes the move

Traditional cross-fade evaluates source and destination during an overlap and weights between them. Unity's [Animation Transitions manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/530/Documentation/Manual/class-Transition.html) documents duration, destination offset, exit time, and interruption policy; its [Blend Trees manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/kr/2018.3/Manual/class-BlendTree.html) distinguishes a state transition from a blend tree and says blend trees are for similar motions with matching timing, such as aligned walk/run foot contacts. The latter point matters: averaging a downswing and a backflip is not the same class of problem as blending two locomotion speeds.

The cost is not just CPU. Holding both sources increases evaluation/state complexity and makes interruption semantics harder; Bollo identifies the double-evaluation worst case. In fast combat, **duration itself is a design cost**: a 100 ms blend is six frames at 60 Hz. This rig's nominal maximum is 80 ms (4.8 frames), and it intentionally uses up to 80% of the next descriptor's pre-active interval (`SpriteRig.ts:705-708`). If the rendered blade is still being averaged toward the new attack when the unchanged hit window becomes live, presentation lags damage. Increasing the duration to hide a bigger discontinuity makes that mismatch worse; repeatedly restarting it makes the character look damped and indecisive.

The current `comboStageTransition` is not a full two-live-clip cross-fade. It retains one rendered snapshot while the new step advances underneath (`SpriteRig.ts:4036-4095`). That is cheaper and authority-safe, but it guarantees position only for captured channels and discards outgoing velocity. **Judgment:** retain a bounded bridge as a fallback, but do not make it longer or ask it to manufacture the character of every pair.

### Motion matching — the wrong scale and the wrong authority model

Simon Clavet's Ubisoft GDC 2016 talk, [“Motion Matching and the Road to Next-Gen Animation”](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023280/Motion-Matching-and-The-Road), describes continuously searching a database for a frame matching both current pose and desired future trajectory, then taking a small blend into that selection. The talk's example imports five or ten minutes of locomotion capture; even then, attack types and defense stances still need manual semantic markup. Epic's [Motion Matching documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine) shows the required pose-search schema, sampled bones/velocities/trajectory, indexed databases, weights, search throttling, reselect history, and blend stack—and warns that more channels cost memory and runtime performance.

**Verdict (my judgment): overkill and actively misaligned.** Dimension Drifters has a deterministic server-selected step from a small authored vocabulary, not a large interchangeable motion corpus. A search system could select a visually close but semantically wrong attack; constraining it so it can never change primitive, step, hand, timing, or impact would reduce it to an expensive transition lookup. It also adds a database authoring/debugging burden to solve a known pairwise seam. Do not propose motion matching for this problem.

### What shipped 2D combat actually demonstrates

- **Skullgirls:** Mariel Cartwright's GDC 2014 [“Fluid and Powerful Animation within Frame Restrictions”](https://media.gdcvault.com/GDC2014/Presentations/Cartwright_Muriel_Animation_Bootcamp_Fluid.pdf) says one anticipation frame can be enough, follow-through fills gaps where in-betweens do not fit, one-frame overshoot gives impact, holds need not have even timing, idle is itself a motion, and an overanimated move with fewer, clearer keys can gain punch. In a first-party [Skullgirls developer post](https://skullgirls.com/2011/05/animation-getting-the-details-right/), Cartwright says fast strong attacks often snap to the active key pose, with hair and clothing flowing afterward; placing the essential pose early also lets the player cancel the remainder without blocking gameplay. This is direct evidence that an intentional “pop” at the strike and authored follow-through after it can be better than universal smoothing.
- **Guilty Gear Xrd:** Arc System Works published Junya Motomura's GDC 2015 [“The X Factor Between 2D and 3D” handout](https://www.ggxrd.com/Motomura_Junya_GuiltyGearXrd.pdf). The hybrid 3D fighter deliberately turns interpolation between keyframes off, then manually adjusts/deforms every visible bone at each key to make poses stronger and less mechanically perfect. That source documents the visual method, not its cancel-state implementation. The relevant lesson is still firm: acclaimed “fluid” combat can be stepped and pose-authored; continuous interpolation is not the goal by itself.
- **Dead Cells:** artist Thomas Vasseur's [production deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-) says attacks are designed pose-to-pose with the fewest convincing keyframes, interpolation frames are added only **before or after** keys and never between them, and VFX carries movement, impact, and strength. The 3D source is exported as 2D PNG frames. The pipeline's main win was cheap retiming and retakes to match gameplay, not runtime averaging. This supports editable authored connective frames around protected contact keys.
- **Hollow Knight:** Nintendo's [Team Cherry interview](https://www.nintendo.com/au/news-and-articles/the-metamorphosis-of-hollow-knight-with-team-cherry-aussie-developer-interview/) establishes traditional hand-drawn, frame-by-frame character animation alongside tightly tuned dodge/dash/slash controls. I found no primary developer source that documents Hollow Knight's runtime attack-link blending, so I will **not** claim it uses authored links or zero-duration blends. It is evidence for a discrete-frame production constraint, not for a specific transition algorithm.
- **Rivals of Aether:** the official [Workshop sprite manual](https://rivalsofaether.com/sprites/) requires fixed-cell strips of ordered frames. The official [Attack Grid reference](https://rivalsofaether.com/category/workshop/page/27/) binds each combat window to explicit animation-frame ranges, hitpause frames, cancel type, and exact cancel frame; pressing at that frame advances to the next window. The documentation does not advertise a cross-fade layer. **Inference:** its published authoring contract is discrete sprite/frame selection with authored timing and cancel edges, so connective readability must live in the chosen frames, holds, effects, and next state's opening—not in 3D-style pose blending.

These sources do **not** prove that every listed game has literally zero blending in every state. They do prove the shippable alternative relevant here: make the contact key immediate and legible, author anticipation/follow-through/holds around protected gameplay frames, and permit exact frame-based cancellation. That is a closer production model for Phaser part sprites than a general-purpose skeletal blend graph.

## Findings and proposal

### Exact seam and target

The highest-confidence seam is step N → N+1 inside `SpriteRig.triggerSwing`: the transition is created at `SpriteRig.ts:5088` on the server-accepted/predicted presentation epoch, the new swing clock replaces the old one at `SpriteRig.ts:5089-5090`, and the first following `animate` frame resets/rebuilds the pose. The desired frame is not merely “same pixels as last frame.” It should inherit the outgoing direction and apparent speed, curve through a short anticipation/link, and arrive at the next attack's authored active pose on its unchanged clock.

### Position continuity is only the floor; velocity continuity is the feel

Plain version: not teleporting means the hand is in the same place on both sides of the cut. Feeling good means the hand is also still **going somewhere**. Imagine a sword tip moving 10 → 20 → 30 pixels across three frames. If the next step begins 30 → 30 → 31, there is no position pop, but the sword visibly hits an invisible brake and starts again. That reads as two cardboard poses. A convincing link continues something like 10 → 20 → 30 → 39 → 46 while its arc bends toward the next key. Bollo's inertialization math explicitly preserves starting velocity; the current smoothstep bridge explicitly has zero slope at its endpoints.

The craft functions are different:

- **Anticipation** is the brief motion opposite or preparatory to the strike. For a fast player attack, it may be only one strong frame; Cartwright's Skullgirls talk says even one is useful. It must fit before the existing active window, not push that window later.
- **Follow-through** is what keeps moving after the contact key: blade path, shoulders, coat, hands, feet, and shadow finish the sentence at different rates. It sells force without delaying input.
- **Settle** redirects remaining energy into a stable ready stance after a chain truly ends. It is not a linear fade of the whole character; heavy channels arrive later, small secondary channels can overshoot and decay.
- **Moving hold** keeps the silhouette readable while refusing to become a still image. The guard can hold, but breathing, hand pressure, blade drift, cloth/head lag, and foot weight continue subtly. Cartwright explicitly warns that idle is motion and should not be eased into and out of as though it were a static pose.

A combo step that settles completely before every next step spends its whole sequence repeatedly stopping and starting. For continued chains, use the outgoing follow-through as the next anticipation. Reserve a full settle for chain expiry, a terminal beat, or a move whose authored character is deliberate stillness.

### The craft decision: authored handoffs first

**Pick: authored linking/carry poses, with the runtime bridge reduced to a guarded fallback.** Do not begin with a full inertialization layer and do not extend the cross-fade.

The current vocabulary already has eight reusable primitives and per-step `intensity`/optional hand routing (`packages/shared/src/melee.ts:118-134`). Give every primitive an authored entry and exit “port”: local weapon angle/offset, body turn/lift, hand spacing, foot plants, shadow, and their outgoing direction. Resolve a transition from the actual ordered pair `(previous primitive + direction + hand) → (next primitive + direction + hand)`. The protected middle of each attack remains byte-for-byte the approved primitive. Only the previous step's post-`followEnd` connective tissue and the next step's pre-`activeStart` anticipation are eligible.

Add one small optional presentation word to the same authored record—name illustrative, not an implementation prescription:

```ts
choreography: {
  primitive: "side-cut",
  intensity: 1.08,
  link?: { mode: "carry" | "settle" | "snap", energy?: number }
}
```

- `carry` means do not return to identity; aim the exit tangent at the next primitive's entry. Use for ordinary accepted N → N+1 links.
- `settle` means finish the overshoot and decay into the weapon's existing ready pose. Use on terminal steps or chain drop. The current 120 ms expiry release is a sensible outer budget, not a requirement to move every channel for all 120 ms.
- `snap` means the next high-priority key owns the frame immediately and any combo residual is killed. Use for authored exceptions and hard interrupts—not as a euphemism for an accidental reset.
- `energy` controls how much outgoing speed survives; it is distinct from `intensity`, which already controls the attack's amplitude. Default it from the primitive pair, then override only the conspicuous bespoke steps.

This keeps quality in the same data the 14 katanas already use, makes local and remote rigs sample the same normalized curve, and gives art direction a stable review surface. It is safer than frame-delta-derived inertialization for this first pass because render cadence differences cannot change the authored tangent. Pair defaults keep the burden bounded: author eight primitive ports and the pair combinations that actually occur, not arbitrary transitions between every pose in the rig.

The existing snapshot bridge still has value as a temporary seatbelt for a small mismatch. Once the full channel owner is corrected, it should either be bypassed for an authored `carry` or limited to the pre-active interval with zero weight on filtered sharp channels. A later inertial residual is worthwhile for remote arrival jitter and unexpected low-priority interruptions, but it should be a separate, measured follow-up.

### Exactly where blending is right and where it is wrong

| Seam | Treatment | What the owner should see |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted same-chain step N → N+1 (`triggerSwing`, bridge request at `SpriteRig.ts:5088`) | **Smooth with authored `carry`**; finish before next `activeStart` | The prior blade arc, shoulder turn, and planted-foot pressure bend into the next anticipation. No return to ready between beats. |
| Normal attack completion → ready/idle; chain expiry at `SpriteRig.ts:8105-8110` | **Authored follow-through, then settle/moving hold** | Weapon overshoots, shoulders and secondary parts catch up, then the existing ready stance breathes; no frozen t=1 card and no instant neutral reset. |
| Ready/idle → attack | **Very short authored anticipation**, potentially one frame; no blanket fade through contact | A readable load precedes the strike, but button-to-danger response remains immediate. |
| Noncombat draw/stow/swap (`beginWeaponSwap`, `SpriteRig.ts:3417-3464`) | **Keep authored flourish/proxy continuity** | The old weapon visibly stows and the new one draws; hands/body flow, while the actual asset identity changes behind that staging instead of morphing. |
| Dual-wield continued chain | **Smooth the acting hand and support-hand reassignment; one shared body carry** | The off hand does not teleport to a new grip, and the torso does not receive two competing blends. |
| Remote observation of the same accepted action | **Short inertial-style residual later, presentation-only** | A late packet changes the local paper pose without a pop, but the accepted impact/VFX still happen on the authoritative phase. Never smooth a true respawn/large root cut. |
| **Strike contact / impact key** | **Sharp**: no cross-fade over the key; use overshoot/hitstop/VFX | The blade arrives decisively. The contact is a punctuation mark, not an average of “before” and “after.” |
| **Successful parry / guard acquisition** (`triggerBrace`, `SpriteRig.ts:5236-5246`) | **Sharp takeover, smooth aftermath** | The guard is present on the parry frame. Repeated parries hold the plateau, as current code intends; arms/body may absorb and settle afterward. |
| **Victim recoil, hit reaction, knockdown, downed/death** | **Sharp impulse/state change, then follow-through** | The body visibly receives the hit now; drag, wobble, cloth, and recovery can decay afterward. Blending away the first recoil frame weakens the hit. |
| **Tumble/roll, slide, dash launch, pound, long-jump launch, emergency crouch/evade** (`applyJumpFeelPose`, `SpriteRig.ts:7184-7268`) | **Hard movement owner wins immediately; kill combo bridge/residual** | The defensive/movement silhouette appears on the actionable frame. The outgoing sword may get a one-frame smear, but it cannot ghost across 80 ms of a roll. The current combo bridge applies after jump/weapon composition (`SpriteRig.ts:10488-10541`), so ownership must be explicit. |
| **Weapon visibility/identity, render-depth sign, sprite/frame swap** | **Discrete**; stage it with surrounding motion, do not interpolate the enum/asset | No half-old/half-new sword, no depth-layer flicker. Position/rotation may link; identity and z-order switch at an authored crossing/occlusion frame. |
| **Authoritative teleport, respawn, scene reset, large correction** | **Snap/rebase** | No rubber-band trail across the arena. “No teleportation” in the ask refers to pose parts, not hiding legitimate world-state cuts. |

The rule is simple: smooth **continuation of intent**; keep **information-bearing events** sharp. Impact, invulnerability/parry acquisition, damage receipt, and hard action ownership are information. Follow-through, recovery, chain handoff, and quiet equipment staging are continuation.

## Cost and risk

- **Preliminary scope:** incremental, not a rewrite. The relevant existing seam is concentrated in the transition helpers/state (`SpriteRig.ts:705-742`, `SpriteRig.ts:4036-4095`), trigger point (`SpriteRig.ts:5088`), final application (`SpriteRig.ts:10541`), and katana sampler/data vocabulary (`melee.ts:118-132`, `melee.ts:292-485`). A data-first improvement can ship per weapon or per step.
- **Authority risk:** low only if all changes remain post-sampling presentation offsets. The accepted `attackSeq`, `swingStart`, descriptor timings, `activeStart/activeEnd/impact/followEnd`, path, and server hit geometry must remain untouched.
- **Hit-readability risk:** high for a blanket or long blend. An 80 ms blend can consume much of a fast step's anticipation; increasing it would visibly delay the weapon even if damage stays on time. Any safety residual must be gone before `activeStart`, and authored links must not move impact/contact frames.
- **Known visual risk:** extending transform capture naively to root rotation can fight tumble, jump, spawn, ultimate, death, or other higher-priority root writers. That ownership decision belongs with the architecture/authority arms; this report defines the visual target and which moments should remain sharp.
- **Estimated code surface, not an implementation measurement:** authored handoffs should touch four concentrated regions rather than the whole rig: shared choreography types/sampling, combo selection/transition capture, the final presentation application, and focused tests. I would budget **well under 200 changed lines inside `SpriteRig.ts` (under 2% of 10,713 lines)** plus `melee.ts` vocabulary/data and tests. Bespoke recipe rows may be numerous, but that is data authoring, not a rig rewrite. `pose-language.ts` already supplies anticipation/active/recovery and should need little or no structural change.
- **Incremental path:** first add an integrated regression that includes root rotation, shadow, and screen-space first difference; then close the present channel-coverage hole; then enable one authored pair on one katana; then expand pair recipes across the 14 weapons; finally evaluate whether remote/interrupt evidence justifies an inertial safety residual. Each stage can be visually reviewed and rolled back independently.
- **Hit timing risk by option:** authored links are low risk if constrained to `followEnd..1` and `0..activeStart`; a longer generic cross-fade is high risk because it can visually overlap the active window; inertialization is medium risk because filtering/ownership must prevent residual weapon motion over impact; motion matching is unacceptable because pose selection itself could diverge from the approved step.
- **Authority risk by option:** deterministic normalized authored links are lowest. A purely visual inertial residual can also be safe but may render differently at different frame cadences, so it needs explicit tests that `attackSeq`, selected step, impact epoch, and hit geometry remain identical. A true two-live-step cross-fade retains more clocks/state and is unnecessary here.
- **Art cost:** medium and real. “End near where the next begins” is not a free algorithm; someone must review silhouette, blade arc, feet, and secondary lag for every pair actually used. The payoff is that this effort is visible, weapon-specific, and tunable without destabilizing gameplay. The Dead Cells postmortem's lesson is apt: cheap retakes against gameplay timing are more valuable than adding generic in-betweens.

## What I would not change

- Do not change the 14 movesets' order, primitive choice, direction, hand, intensity, beats, cadence, damage, path/ribbon, `activeStart`, `activeEnd`, `impact`, or `followEnd`. Connective tissue must wrap the approved action, not redesign it.
- Do not change `comboStepForChain` or derive a second local combo clock. The retained accepted sequence and weapon/family expiry logic at `melee.ts:1949-1985` is the correct shared decision.
- Do not move player world position or server hit geometry to chase a visual pose. Katana root rotation/lift is paper presentation; authoritative root translation remains off limits.
- Do not smooth impact, parry confirmation, hit recoil, downed/death entry, roll/tumble takeover, z-order/depth switches, respawn, or large network corrections. Their sharpness communicates gameplay.
- Do not lengthen the global 80 ms bridge. It already occupies up to 80% of the next pre-active interval and cannot solve velocity or bad authoring by becoming slower.
- Do not replace the existing rest stances. `c072111` deliberately preserved them, and `pose-language.ts` already treats idle as a moving family equilibrium. Link to those stances on a true stop.
- Do not replace the established stow/draw proxy flourish with a generic blend, and do not interpolate weapon identity. The current staging is more readable and avoids ghost assets.
- Do not introduce motion matching or rewrite `SpriteRig.ts` into a skeletal graph for this issue.

## Verdict and visible target

**Is my angle worth shipping first? No—not before the measured seam/ownership fix and its authority guard.** The root/shadow omission can still rotate the whole card discontinuously, so authored elegance built on top of it would be wasted or misdiagnosed. The measurement/architecture/authority arms should first make every presentation channel continuous on the accepted frame and prove the server clock/hit window did not move.
