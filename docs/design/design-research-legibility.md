# Design Research: Stat and Weapon Legibility

> **Mandate and scope (research arm, 2026-07-22):** Establish what shipped bullet-heaven and action-roguelite games actually expose, where they expose it, which simplifications worked or failed, how players respond to numerical versus behavioural stats and automatic allocation, and what comparable catalog-management mechanisms imply for Dimension Drifters. This is an evidence report, not an implementation. I verified the supplied code claims directly, distinguish primary evidence from community reaction and my own judgment, and finish with one concrete, visualizable recommendation plus its costs—even though the panel role is principally factual.

## Counting rules and method

- “Exposed stat count” means named player-facing character/combat statistics visible without reverse-engineering; I will separately record permanent-HUD, level-up/shop, inventory, pause/details, and hidden/derived information.
- Counts can differ by platform, patch, character, equipment, or whether zero-value rows are suppressed. Every count will therefore carry a version/date or an explicit counting rule where the source permits it.
- Store pages, official wikis/manuals, patch notes, developer talks/posts, and direct inspection of this repository are treated as factual sources. Player comments are evidence of reaction, not proof of universal preference.
- The only file I will modify is this report.

## Plain-English summary

Five stats are not too many: successful games expose 15–21 when the numbers are shown where choices happen, while games that hide math stay readable by giving players a tiny active loadout, stable tags and memorable weapon behaviours. Dimension Drifters’ harder problem is that five attributes feed grades, requirements, crit, class bonuses and pairing across 326 weapons; the current UI already discloses its automatic ballast point, but that point still controls one third of attribute income without being the player’s choice. I would first ship a “Build Lens” that shows exact before→after results and makes class → delivery → element the catalog’s main grammar, with every weapon led by one behavioural sentence. That needs a presentation audit of all 326 weapons but roughly zero structural retagging and no immediate rebalance. I would not delete stats or hide ballast before that test; if ballast still feels like the game correcting the player, change it separately, because shipped precedents make lowest-stat automation optional or move automatic growth outside the choice.

## What I actually verified in the codebase

> Snapshot note: this is the live shared worktree on `feat/v0.118-metagame` on 2026-07-22. Other panel work was already modifying weapon files, so these are observations, not claims about an immutable commit. I made no product-code changes.

### Attribute and level-up law

- There are exactly five network/gameplay attributes, in stable tie-break order: `str`, `dex`, `int`, `con`, `luk`. The source describes them as melee, finesse/ranged, caster+signature, survivability, and luck respectively ([`packages/shared/src/leveling.ts:34`](../../packages/shared/src/leveling.ts#L34)).
- The run caps at level 30. Each resolved level-up produces three points: +2 chosen and +1 ballast ([`leveling.ts:19`](../../packages/shared/src/leveling.ts#L19), [`leveling.ts:23`](../../packages/shared/src/leveling.ts#L23)). The ballast destination is calculated *after* the chosen +2 and goes to the current lowest attribute; equal lows resolve by the `STR → DEX → INT → CON → LUK` declaration order ([`leveling.ts:45`](../../packages/shared/src/leveling.ts#L45), [`packages/server/src/rooms/progression.ts:72`](../../packages/server/src/rooms/progression.ts#L72)). One character quirk can instead make ballast follow the chosen stat ([`progression.ts:73`](../../packages/server/src/rooms/progression.ts#L73)).
- A concrete server test pins the ordinary result: starting at 1/1/1/1/1 and choosing STR yields 3/2/1/1/1, because DEX wins the post-choice low-stat tie ([`packages/server/src/rooms/progression.test.ts:31`](../../packages/server/src/rooms/progression.test.ts#L31)). Across the 29 earned level-ups, total allocated income is 87 points; the test pins a mono-STR ceiling of 62 and an all-stat sum of 97 for the tested non-Drifter spread ([`progression.test.ts:176`](../../packages/server/src/rooms/progression.test.ts#L176)).
- **Correction to the supplied shorthand “silently auto-assigns”: the allocation is automatic, but the current client explicitly previews it.** Its model emits the literal receipt `+2 [CHOSEN] (YOU) • +1 [LOWEST] (BALLAST)` on every choice ([`packages/client/src/ui/level-up-model.ts:98`](../../packages/client/src/ui/level-up-model.ts#L98)); the level-up header says `PICK +2 • LOWEST +1 BALLAST` ([`level-up-model.ts:421`](../../packages/client/src/ui/level-up-model.ts#L421)); and the five-second timer names the exact timeout allocation ([`packages/client/src/scenes/ArenaScene.ts:8097`](../../packages/client/src/scenes/ArenaScene.ts#L8097)). The screen presents all five attribute choices and previews resulting held-source damage, HP/regen, crit, requirement recovery, or the honest warning `NO HELD-WEAPON SCALING` ([`level-up-model.ts:206`](../../packages/client/src/ui/level-up-model.ts#L206), [`ArenaScene.ts:8621`](../../packages/client/src/scenes/ArenaScene.ts#L8621)).
- If the player times out, the server chooses the held weapon’s best grade-scaling attribute; absent grades fall back to CON ([`leveling.ts:71`](../../packages/shared/src/leveling.ts#L71), [`packages/server/src/rooms/GameRoom.ts:9698`](../../packages/server/src/rooms/GameRoom.ts#L9698)). Stacked missed choices are drained in one pass rather than granting repeated invulnerability windows.

### What the five labels actually control

- CON is the simplest mapping: each point above baseline adds 8 maximum HP and 0.7 HP/s regeneration ([`leveling.ts:98`](../../packages/shared/src/leveling.ts#L98), [`leveling.ts:130`](../../packages/shared/src/leveling.ts#L130)).
- Crit is a two-input derived stat: 5% base, +2 percentage points per LUK above 1, +0.8 points per DEX above 1, capped at 75%; a crit deals 2× ([`leveling.ts:102`](../../packages/shared/src/leveling.ts#L102)). LUK also enters loot/harvest presentation, while DEX may or may not scale the held weapon; the card distinguishes those cases ([`level-up-model.ts:225`](../../packages/client/src/ui/level-up-model.ts#L225)).
- Damage is not a fixed “STR = damage” table. Every weapon or individual damage source can carry S/A/B/C/D/E grades for any subset of the five attributes ([`packages/shared/src/weapons.ts:755`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L755), [`weapons.ts:1225`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L1225)). Per-point coefficients range from 10% at S to 1.5% at E, sum additively across graded attributes, and ungraded legacy weapons default to STR B ([`weapons.ts:1193`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L1193)). Requirements can separately penalize under-statted weapons by 12% per missing point, floored at 25% ([`weapons.ts:761`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L761)). Thus “five attributes” generates substantially more than five player-facing relationships.

### Weapon-catalog and loadout law

- The source has a runtime invariant for **326 active + 9 archived = 335 durable weapon IDs**, excluding fists ([`packages/shared/src/weapon-resource.ts:284`](../../packages/shared/src/weapon-resource.ts#L284), [`weapon-resource.ts:314`](../../packages/shared/src/weapon-resource.ts#L314)). Archiving is deliberately identity-preserving: definitions remain resolvable, disappear from acquisition/presentation rosters, and owned copies are converted to Scrip ([`weapons.ts:782`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L782), [`packages/shared/src/bank.ts:543`](../../packages/shared/src/bank.ts#L543)).
- Every weapon already has a structured taxonomy: grip (1H/2H/dual/mounted), size, delivery, fire mode, element, one of three class pools, family, range band, scaling labels, and optional handling tags ([`weapons.ts:741`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L741)). It also carries base combat parameters and may carry rarity/affix on its *instance*, graded scaling and requirements, distinct delivery blocks, and bespoke secondary effects. This is valuable authoring structure, but it is not automatically a player-legible hierarchy.
- The active run model is three equipped slots plus overflow bag ([`packages/shared/src/state.ts:178`](../../packages/shared/src/state.ts#L178)). The persistent bank starts at 72 entries and can expand by six shelves of 12 to 144; expedition carry is three active physical slots plus a base 12-slot pack, with at most 16 physical weapons because a bound pair consumes two ([`packages/shared/src/bank.ts:5`](../../packages/shared/src/bank.ts#L5)).
- Two genuinely different 1H weapons can pair only when class pool and live delivery lane match; beams, throws, authored duals, ground zones, and fists are excluded ([`weapons.ts:1112`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L1112)). A pair is not a bonus fourth slot. Pair throughput is capped and same-family pairs receive separate tuning ([`weapons.ts:1147`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L1147)).
- A second composition rule rewards carrying two same-class weapons with +8% class damage and three with +18% ([`weapons.ts:1166`](../../packages/shared/src/weapons.ts#L1166)). The level-up context surfaces the current class count/bonus, but this makes class simultaneously a loot filter, pairing constraint, and set-building signal ([`level-up-model.ts:404`](../../packages/client/src/ui/level-up-model.ts#L404)).
- Signature augments are a separate constrained-choice system: a server-authored three-card draft every fifth level, gated to universal parry plus the captured held-delivery lane ([`packages/shared/src/augments.ts:198`](../../packages/shared/src/augments.ts#L198), [`augments.ts:261`](../../packages/shared/src/augments.ts#L261)). This matters because players are already alternating between a five-card numerical allocation decision and occasional three-card behavioural choices.

**Codebase implication to carry into the research:** Dimension Drifters does not merely have “five stats and 326 weapons.” It has five allocation labels feeding per-source graded scaling, dual-input crit, requirements, loot, ultimate attunement, and survivability; and a 326-item catalog feeding three class-set thresholds, pairing compatibility, rarity/affix identity, inventory risk, and several delivery behaviours. The comparison set must therefore distinguish *count* from *relationship density* and *where exact consequences are shown*.

### Why Brotato can expose 16–17 stats and still read cleanly

Brotato’s count is high, but each row earns a stable icon, a stable location and a mostly one-sentence rule. More importantly, the game does not ask the player to cross-reference all 16 against 326 weapons in real time. It pauses between short waves; pins the current stat rail beside no more than four offers; writes item trade-offs in the same vocabulary; limits the loadout to six; groups weapons by class and tier; and biases early shops toward things that fit the owned build. “+8 Range, −3% Speed” can be evaluated without knowing a hidden grade table. The production/design cost is a highly disciplined content schema: every shop item must be authored and balanced as explicit movement on shared rows, every weapon needs class/tier membership, and shop generation needs compatibility weighting. Public sources do not disclose studio-hours or budget for that work, so it would be false precision to invent a dollar cost.

### What Vampire Survivors shows instead of weapon math

Vampire Survivors does not actually hide its entire character sheet: it puts 21 aggregates on character select and pause. What it largely hides is *per-weapon internal math and derived DPS*. It substitutes six persistent weapon icons with visible levels, six passive icons, a small level-up hand, evolution recipes, conspicuous projectile/cooldown feedback and end-of-run damage totals. The player can say “Bible + Spellbinder evolved into Vespers” without ever calculating the exact Area/cooldown interaction. That is compression by **named objects and recipes**, not by having few underlying stats.

### Is this a stat-count problem or a presentation problem?

**Usually presentation first, but not presentation only.** Three pieces of evidence are hard to reconcile with a pure count thesis:

1. Brotato remains playable with 16–17 primary rows because the full rail is visible at the shop where exact trade-offs occur.
2. Halls of Torment and DRG: Survivor carry 15 aggregate rows, but each moved or keeps exact source statistics on the selection/pause surface; neither pins 15 rows over combat.
3. Hades and Risk of Rain 2 expose almost no aggregate math yet support deep builds by making the *owned objects* persistent and choices local. Conversely, both Risk of Rain 2’s ItemStats mod and 20 Minutes Till Dawn’s BetterUI demonstrate a recurring demand for exact details once the default presentation withholds too much.

Stat count becomes the primary problem when rows cease to be independent concepts and become a relationship-exam: one choice alters several differently weighted weapons, a second stat also feeds crit, requirements can apply a separate penalty, and the answer changes with the held source. Megabonk’s 31-row vocabulary plus player spreadsheet requests is a warning. The evidence therefore supports this test: **if a player can see one candidate’s exact before→after consequence beside the candidate, keep the underlying depth; if they must remember a formula or inspect several remote screens, reduce or group the relationship.** Dimension Drifters already attempts the first half on its attribute card, but the grade/requirement/class/pair/affix layers can still make the weapon catalog a count problem because 326 objects participate in several overlapping taxonomies.

## Documented simplification events—and what they actually cost

### Diablo III: fewer permanent point decisions, more authored skill behaviours

The popular summary loses two different redesigns.

First, Blizzard’s January 2012 systems post replaced Attack, Defense and Precision as core attributes with the class-targeted Strength, Dexterity, Intellect and Vitality model. Director Jay Wilson said the goals were more intuitive stats, easier recognition of off-class junk, less item overlap and a cleaner item pool; he also explicitly called out the **“far reaching requirements to re-itemize and balance the game.”** Stats were moved onto the inventory surface so equipping an item immediately changed the displayed sheet ([Blizzard, “Systems Changes”](https://www.bluetracker.gg/diablo3/topic/us-en/4325959-systems-changes/)). Shipped characters gained core attributes automatically rather than receiving Diablo II-style points to spend.

Second, Beta Patch 13 removed runestones as ranked inventory objects. Skills and runes unlocked by level; every active skill got **5 runes**; rune ranks disappeared; and six categorized skill slots were linked directly to the six action-bar positions. Blizzard’s explanation is unusually frank: unrestricted “choose anything” presentation had failed to communicate the combat roles the designers already used internally, while itemized runes had become the project’s largest design hurdle. The studio chose one strong effect per rune rather than multiple ranks and accepted that some players would mourn rune items ([Jay Wilson, “Skill and Rune Changes”](https://www.bluetracker.gg/diablo3/topic/us-en/4475014-skill-and-rune-changes/), [Beta Patch 13 notes](https://www.bluetracker.gg/diablo3/topic/us-en/3123246179-diablo-iii-beta-patch-18-notes-updated-4-19/)).

That was **complexity transfer, not pure deletion**. Five launch classes × roughly 20–25 active skills each × five runes implies on the order of **500–625 authored rune variants**, plus base skills, VFX, tooltips and balance. Blizzard removed inventory/rank combinatorics and permanent mistakes, then paid for behavioural breadth. A contemporary PC Gamer review captures the mixed response in one place: it lamented the disappearance of permanent character choices and found much loot abstract, yet gave the game 90/100 and described skill/rune combinations as personal, tactical and highly experimentable ([Tom Francis, *PC Gamer*, 2012](https://www.pcgamer.com/diablo-3-review/)). IGN likewise praised the lack of regretted point allocations and greater experimentation, as recorded in the [contemporary reception summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diablo_III#Critical_reception). The honest finding is **mixed**: respec freedom and behaviour were a success for experimentation; some players felt the character became a loadout and gear became a class-number multiplier rather than an authored identity.

### Dead Cells Update 19: a small stat deletion required a run-wide rebalance

Motion Twin’s 2020 *Update of Plenty* removed bonus stat scrolls from `++` and `S` gear. The stated reason was that gear level had lost importance and players lacked incentive to switch equipment. Compensation was not a tooltip edit: the studio added scrolls to biomes, reduced enemy damage, and changed gear damage to scale with item level. In the same update it removed gold scaling for graspable numbers, reduced over-common conditional-damage affixes because every item was acquiring three or four status synergies, and changed shop categories from equipment type to Brutality/Tactics/Survival because the growing pool made the old categories less useful ([official Update 19 notes](https://dead-cells.com/patchnotes/19)).

This is the clearest cost warning for Dimension Drifters. Removing one source of stats changed enemy damage, biome rewards, item-level curves, shop filters and legendary handling. Motion Twin also deliberately preserved or strengthened behavioural identity—new distinct DoT rules, teleports on the War Javelin, two-handed crossbow actions—while cleaning numerical clutter. Player feedback during the alpha was split on early-run difficulty, and subsequent point releases retuned damage/health repeatedly; the official notes record those follow-ups rather than pretending the first pass landed perfectly.

### Diablo IV Loot Reborn: fewer affixes, with complexity relocated to crafting

In Season 4 Blizzard cut dropped Legendary items to **3 affixes** and Rares to **2**, increased their magnitude, replaced narrow conditions such as damage to non-injured Elites with readable base effects, and reduced total item volume. Its public goal was to make upgrades faster to recognize and spend less time sorting. Customization moved into Tempering and Masterworking, including milestone boosts every fourth Masterwork tier ([Blizzard, “Loot Reborn”](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/24077223/galvanize-your-legend-in-season-4-loot-reborn)). Again, the shipped precedent is not “remove depth”; it is **simple drop evaluation, optional downstream optimization**. The cost was a full affix/item/drop/material/crafting rebalance, not just reducing lines on a card.

### Where simplification crossed the identity line

- In a later Diablo IV PTR, Blizzard reduced every Unique type to **one guaranteed affix** to create more roll freedom. Player feedback said this diluted Unique identity and removed a predictable anchor for build concepts, so Blizzard reverted upward to **two guaranteed affixes** before release. This is unusually direct studio evidence that fewer fixed properties can make a catalog *less* legible: a named item stops guaranteeing what its name means ([Blizzard’s Unique-affix explanation and reversion](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24268702/hunt-the-death-cult-in-season-of-death-awakening)).
- Destiny 2 launched Year 1 weapons with fixed perk rolls, making each named gun easy to catalogue but making duplicate drops functionally solved. Bungie’s Year 2/Forsaken system restored random rolls and “more meaningful” weapon mods ([Bungie, 9 August 2018](https://www.bungie.net/7/en/News/article/47079)); its earlier state-of-the-game plan had already promised randomly selected, rerollable stat bonuses ([Bungie, 2017](https://www.bungie.net/7/en/News/article/46504)). This is outside bullet-heaven, so it is boundary evidence rather than a template. Its lesson is narrower: catalog simplification fails when duplicates and discovery are core rewards and the simplified objects no longer give collectors a reason to care.

## What makes a stat feel good: number changes versus behaviour changes

There is no controlled, genre-wide study showing “players prefer behaviours by X%.” The evidence available is shipped-system structure, developer intent, reviews and community language. It points to different jobs for the two kinds of upgrade.

**Numerical upgrades are good infrastructure.** Damage, cooldown, health and speed provide smooth pacing, permit frequent rewards and make near-term comparisons possible. Brotato makes them satisfying by exposing a stable before/after rail and meaningful negatives. Dead Cells uses three colored scaling lanes so a number also signals which gear belongs to the build. Players usually discuss these as a target or breakpoint—enough armor, capped dodge, one more projectile—rather than as a memorable run story.

**Behavioural upgrades are better identity anchors.** Diablo III’s contemporary review did not remember “+12% Disintegrate damage”; it described a wide two-hand beam versus a main beam surrounded by small defensive rays. DRG: Survivor spaces ordinary stat cards around named Overclocks at weapon levels 6/12/18. Vampire Survivors turns a base weapon/passive recipe into a named evolution. Hades players name the god, action slot and Duo boon. These choices are easier to recount because they change what the player does or what appears on screen. The cost is much higher: distinct behavior needs implementation, VFX/audio, interactions, localization and bespoke balance, while `+10% damage` reuses a formula.

The boundary is not perfectly binary. A number becomes behavioural when it crosses a perceptible threshold: +1 projectile, 100% pierce, a cooldown low enough to maintain an aura, or enough movement speed to change positioning. Conversely, a supposedly exotic behaviour that is merely “deal another hidden packet of damage” will be remembered as a number. **My synthesis, not a sourced universal law:** a good stat has a predictable verb (“fires faster”), an immediately visible consequence, a reason to prefer it over another axis, and either a meaningful threshold or synergy. A bad stat requires formula recall, duplicates another stat’s job, or changes several unrelated systems without showing the aggregate result.

### Path of Exile versus Last Epoch: depth placement matters

Path of Exile’s official passive tree currently advertises **1,325 nodes**. Grinding Gear Games uses named Notables to summarize clusters and Keystones with one upside/one downside to change fundamental rules ([official passive tree](https://www.pathofexile.com/passive-skill-tree)). That creates extraordinary cross-class expressiveness, but the visible possibility space is itself the onboarding cost.

Last Epoch localizes complexity: its developers describe a hard budget of **five specialized skills**, each intended to remain an active tool rather than an auto-cast passive ([Eleventh Hour Games FAQ](https://forum.lastepoch.com/t/official-last-epoch-general-faq/47963/1)). Skill-specific trees and scaling tags put relevant transformations beside the skill. That is not proof that Last Epoch is “better”—PoE deliberately sells global combinatorial freedom—but it explains the readability gap often reported by players: Last Epoch asks “how will *this skill* change?” while PoE asks “where in the global network can this character travel?” Dimension Drifters’ per-source grades currently lean toward the latter relationship cost without displaying a navigable global map.

## How large catalogs stay legible

No successful comparison simply shows its whole catalog every level. The recurring mechanisms are concrete:

1. **Hard simultaneous limits.** Hades commits to one of six weapons pre-run; 20 Minutes Till Dawn to one of 11; Megabonk to four; DRG: Survivor to four on a fixed schedule; Brotato and Vampire Survivors to six; Soulstone Survivors to six despite 373 active skills. The player learns a hand, not a database.
2. **Stable functional nouns.** Hades uses five action slots and gods. Brotato uses class tags and four tiers. Dead Cells uses three colors. Soulstone uses skill tags. Risk of Rain 2 uses item-tier colors and stack icons. These are not decorative filters: drops, upgrades and synergies use the same nouns.
3. **Pool steering.** Brotato’s early shops guarantee weapons and later rolls favor the owned weapon/class. Soulstone’s character/weapon/type pool plus reroll/banish/lock prevents 373 skills from becoming one flat lottery. Vampire Survivors gives reroll/skip/banish/seal.
4. **Compression through transformation.** Brotato combines identical tiers. Vampire Survivors evolves a maxed weapon through a passive recipe. DRG: Survivor turns weapon-level milestones into Overclocks. A player tracks the destination of an owned object instead of continually replacing it with an unrelated ID.
5. **Details on demand outside danger.** Exact weapon/source math goes on pause, shop, level-up or inventory; the combat HUD carries the small active set. Risk of Rain 2’s tooltip mods show the downside of making details *too* remote, but its one-pickup-at-a-time interaction shows why 187 items need not become 187 simultaneous choices.
6. **Guaranteed identity before random variation.** Diablo IV’s Unique-affix reversion and Destiny 2’s fixed-roll retreat point in opposite directions but share a rule: a named item needs a stable reason to be named; random rolls may vary it, but should not erase its recognizable verb.

Dimension Drifters already stores most of the machinery needed for this. A read-only census of the literal catalog metadata found **3 class pools, 9 delivery labels, 8 elements, 3 range bands, 4 grips and 58 family strings** across active/inactive definitions and schema examples. The precise active roster is the code-pinned 326. The problem is not missing metadata; it is that class, delivery, family, grip, range, element, grades and requirements are all candidate primary explanations. Comparable games pick one or two as the player’s first sentence and demote the rest to details.

## Hidden and automatic allocation: the closest shipped precedents

### The exact lowest-stat algorithm does exist—but as an opt-in

Habitica’s “Flat” auto-allocation assigns each new point to the stat with the **fewest points**, with a documented tie order. That is the closest exact match I found to `lowestAttr`; it also offers class-weighted and activity-weighted modes. The decisive difference is consent: automatic allocation is a setting for users who find build allocation distracting, and they can switch back to manual for future points ([Habitica automatic-allocation documentation](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Automatic_Allocation)). It demonstrates that the algorithm is shippable, not that a mandatory one-third tax is liked.

Other shipped games automate growth only after removing it from the advertised choice budget:

- Risk of Rain 2 automatically increases three base stats every level; player agency lives in item pickups.
- Diablo III automatically grows core attributes; agency lives in skills/runes and gear. Player response remains split between freedom to experiment and loss of permanent character authorship, as the review evidence above shows.
- Final Fantasy XIV 1.x publicly described automated character development by class level, then later Stormblood Patch 4.0 removed manual attribute points entirely and reworked related stats/gear. The official patch notes also removed the allocation UI and the reset item rather than presenting an unchosen point as if it were a choice ([2011 automation plan](https://forum.square-enix.com/ffxiv/threads/11070-dev1072-Abolition-of-Physical-Levels-Attribute-Point-Revisions), [Patch 4.0 notes](https://na.finalfantasyxiv.com/lodestone/topics/detail/296b070f41c0e0dedae24de50ae7c51abfa95055)).

I did **not** find a shipped bullet-heaven/action-roguelite that says “choose two points, then I will visibly spend a third on your current lowest stat” as a mandatory every-level loop. Absence from this search is not proof none exists; it is a genuine research gap. Habitica is the exact algorithmic analog, but belongs to a different genre and makes the mode optional.

### How players react when the game spends points for them

Public reaction is mixed for a consistent reason. Players accept invisible baseline growth when no point is placed in their hand: Risk of Rain 2 does not tell the player “you earned three allocatable stats” and then take them away. Players object when an automatic system occupies the semantic space of authorship. A Diablo IV forum thread about automatic attributes contains both sides in plain terms: some players describe the missing allocation as lost build definition and a weaker level-up; others call the old pattern an illusion because most builds meet requirements and dump the rest into one dominant stat ([representative Blizzard-forum discussion](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/d4/t/automatic-attributes-why/5558)). This is qualitative reaction, not a vote.

Dimension Drifters’ ballast is currently explicit rather than hidden, which prevents surprise but highlights the agency conflict. Across 29 earned levels it controls **29 of 87 points—exactly one third of run attribute income**. It also reacts to the chosen +2, so the receipt can produce a stat the player did not anticipate without simulating the post-choice tie. That makes it neither harmless baseline growth nor a full choice. Its systemic benefit is real—bounded specialization and predictable minimum spread simplify balance—but the research offers no evidence that making players watch an algorithm correct their build every level feels good. The closest successful implementations either make automation optional (Habitica) or move it entirely outside the choice transaction (Risk of Rain 2, Diablo III, FFXIV).

## What the evidence says

These are the conclusions I would hand the other Sols as constraints, not aesthetic preferences:

1. **Do not justify a stat cut with the number five.** Successful games expose 15–21 aggregate rows, sometimes 31, when the relevant consequence is beside the decision. Dimension Drifters’ problem is relationship density and inconsistent priority, not raw attribute count.
2. **Keep combat HUD sparse; put exact math on level-up, weapon compare, inventory and pause.** No comparison game pins its full 15–21-row sheet over bullet combat. Halls of Torment’s 1.0 move to put source stats on the selection screen is the most directly applicable precedent.
3. **A candidate must answer “what changes now?”** Showing `DEX +2` is weaker than `Starfall Rifle 137→154; crit 21→22.6%`. The current client is already closer to the right answer than the owner’s shorthand suggests; preserve and standardize that preview.
4. **Use numbers for pacing and behaviours for identity.** Frequent `+damage/+speed/+health` can remain, but the memorable milestones should alter a weapon’s verb, targeting, projectile count, movement or status rule. This is where authoring budget should go.
5. **A 326-item catalog needs a small player-facing grammar.** Pick one first-level hierarchy, restrict each run’s exposure, steer pools toward the owned build, and guarantee a named weapon’s core identity. Tags that exist only in data do not solve player comprehension.
6. **Do not hide mandatory point spending.** Either call ballast baseline growth outside the choice, make an automatic mode opt-in, or let the player own the point. The current explicit receipt is better than secrecy, but there is no comparable evidence for a mandatory reactive one-third allocation.
7. **Every mechanical simplification has a compensating cost.** Dead Cells’ gear-stat cut touched enemies, biome scrolls, shops and item curves; Diablo IV’s affix cut created new crafting systems. A “simple” removal of grades, requirements or ballast would require catalog-wide simulation and rebalance.

## Concrete proposal: the Build Lens, not a stat-ectomy

My evidence-led option is deliberately narrower than deleting systems: keep the five-attribute simulation and the 326 durable identities, but give players one consistent reading order everywhere. Make **class → delivery → element** the weapon’s primary grammar; show exact owned-loadout consequences at decision time; demote family, grades, requirements, pairing rules and raw affix machinery into progressively disclosed rows. Keep the full sheet off the combat HUD.

This is what the level-up screen should literally look like (example numbers illustrative; labels and ordering are the proposal):

```text
LEVEL 12 — CHOOSE ONE FOCUS (+2)

YOUR BUILD     HP 152  •  REGEN 4.2/s  •  CRIT 21.0% ×2
HELD LOADOUT   STARFALL RIFLE [RANGED • PROJECTILE • SHOCK]

┌ FINESSE  +2 DEX ────────────────┐
│ Starfall Rifle damage  137 → 154 │
│ Sidearm damage          92 → 101 │
│ Critical chance       21 → 22.6% │
│ AUTO BALANCE AFTER PICK: +1 INT  │
└───────────────────────────────────┘

┌ VITALITY  +2 CON ───────────────┐
│ Max health             152 → 168 │
│ Regeneration          4.2 → 5.6/s│
│ Held-weapon damage     no change │
│ AUTO BALANCE AFTER PICK: +1 INT  │
└───────────────────────────────────┘

[ STR ] [ DEX ] [ INT ] [ CON ] [ LUK ]       HOLD DETAILS: formulas/grades
```

All five choices remain available; five is within the shipped comparison range, and hiding two would obstruct planned weapon switches. The compact row lets a practiced player choose quickly, while focus/hover opens the full card. A non-scaling card should say `NO EQUIPPED SOURCE USES STR` rather than sell an abstract bonus. The exact automatic destination must appear **per candidate**, because the post-choice lowest stat can change. Rename “ballast” in player-facing copy to the plain `AUTO BALANCE`; keep “ballast” as an internal design term if useful.

The weapon card should lead with behaviour and fit, not taxonomy soup:

```text
STARFALL RIFLE                                      RARE
RANGED  ›  PROJECTILE  ›  SHOCK

CHAIN FIRE — Bolts jump to 2 nearby enemies.

FIT         DEX A  •  LUK C
IF EQUIPPED Damage 137 → 154   Crit 21%   Range LONG
LOADOUT     Ranged set 2/3: +8%   •   Pair: compatible with Arc Pistol

[SHOW DETAILS]
Requirement DEX 6 • affix roll • exact cooldown • source grades • durability
```

The arsenal browser should open to three class sections, then offer the nine delivery labels as filters. Element and range are chips; the current 58 family strings are search/details vocabulary, not 58 top-level categories. The screen should never present “326 weapons” as one flat grid. Its default sort should be `works with current build`, with explicit reasons such as `DEX A`, `completes 3/3 Ranged`, or `pairs with equipped pistol`; a second sort preserves collector/archive order.

The only combat-HUD additions should be the three active weapon identities/cooldowns and any behavior-critical state. No STR/DEX/INT/CON/LUK rail belongs over the arena. Pause and inventory get the full Build Lens: five attributes, three result aggregates (damage by equipped source, survivability, crit/find), and expandable formula detail.

### What I would test before touching the law

The proposal keeps current ballast behavior in the first legibility test because presentation changes are independently testable and avoid confounding balance. Test whether a new player can answer, without a wiki: “Why did DEX help?”, “Where did the automatic point go?”, “Why is this weapon a fit?”, and “Will these two weapons pair?” If the automatic point remains the comprehension or agency failure after the exact receipt is visible, the evidence-supported follow-up is a separate experiment: **move +1 into a predictable baseline-growth schedule or an opt-in Balanced mode**, rather than making it more hidden. I would not jump straight to +3 chosen points; that changes caps, requirements and every grade curve.

## What this costs

### Content and production cost

- **Retagging:** structurally, approximately **0 of 326** active weapons need new tags for the proposed first-level grammar; `classPool`, `delivery`, `element`, range and family are required fields already. That is not zero work. **326 of 326 need a presentation audit** to ensure the generated labels tell the truth, and every weapon needs a stable one-sentence behavioural identity. If family aliases are normalized through a crosswalk, the current census suggests at most **58 family mappings**, rather than rewriting 326 records.
- **UI/content authoring:** five level-up templates need a consistent exact-delta contract across held sources, ultimate/loot effects and no-scaling cases. Weapon compare needs class/set/pair reasons in one place. Nine delivery icons/labels and eight element labels need a controlled vocabulary. All 326 cards require layout QA at long names, multiple grades, requirements and affixes.
- **Balance:** the Build Lens itself changes no numbers, so it should require **no weapon or enemy rebalance**. It will expose balance defects that were previously obscure. Those findings should be fixed as defects, not counted as a reason to keep the UI vague.
- **Verification:** because damage can exist per source rather than per weapon, exact comparison must validate every distinct damage source, not only 326 top-level IDs. Pair caps, class bonuses, under-requirement penalties and crit inputs need automated before/after tests to prevent a truthful-looking but false card.

### What depth or expressiveness is lost

No simulation depth is deliberately removed. The cost is information hierarchy: an expert needs one extra expansion to see family, requirement math, source grades and affix detail, and a primary class/delivery label may understate deliberately hybrid weapons. A behavioural sentence also creates a maintenance obligation whenever mechanics change. Those are real costs, but smaller than invalidating persisted weapon identities or flattening 326 balance profiles.

### What would break under the tempting deeper cuts

If a later option deletes grades or merges STR/DEX/INT, assume **all 326 active weapons plus every per-source grade block** need re-scaling, not a sample. Requirements must be rewritten or removed; timeout allocation loses its “best scaling attribute” rule; five level-up cards and tests change; crit’s DEX contribution changes; ultimate attunement/loot interactions need review; and 40 legacy characters may lose intended profiles. If ballast is deleted or converted to player-owned points, 29 points move at cap: mono-stat ceilings, CON health/regen, LUK/DEX crit, requirements and the tested all-stat sum all change. Dead Cells shows why compensation would reach enemies and reward pacing, not only attributes.

## What I would not change

- **Do not reduce five attributes merely to hit a fashionable count.** Five is already below every stat-forward comparable, and CON has an admirably clear contract. Fix cross-stat and per-source explanation before removing labels.
- **Do not remove behavioural augments.** Three-card drafts every fifth level are closer to the memorable Overclock/rune/boon pattern than the numerical attribute loop. If anything receives more authoring budget, it should be these milestones.
- **Do not flatten the weapon catalog into cosmetic skins.** The owner has paid for 326 durable identities; Diablo IV’s Unique reversion and Destiny’s fixed-roll retreat show the identity risk. Archive weak or redundant entries deliberately, but preserve IDs and collector receipts.
- **Do not remove the three-slot limit or class thresholds merely for UI convenience.** These are useful compression rules. Pre-made duals remain one authored weapon and one slot; explain that at the weapon decision.
- **Do not put the full sheet on the combat HUD.** The comparison set is remarkably consistent here: decisions can pause and explain; combat needs silhouettes, cooldowns, health and threats.
- **Do not make ballast quieter than it is now.** If it remains mandatory, show the exact destination. “Automatic but disclosed” is defensible; “spent without asking or telling” is not.

## Honest verdict

**Yes, I would ship the Build Lens before deleting stats or weapons.** It attacks the evidence-backed failure mode—remote and competing explanations—without forcing a 326-item rebalance or erasing content identity. I would *not* claim that it vindicates ballast: one third of attribute income remains automatic, and the closest exact precedent makes that algorithm opt-in. If observed players still describe the automatic point as the game undoing their choice after this surface is truthful, I would then replace the reactive ballast law in a separately balanced change. I would not combine that experiment with the presentation rewrite, because a cleaner screen and a new progression curve failing together would teach the team nothing.

## Source-quality and uncertainty notes

- Official developer posts/patch notes are used for intent and change history; community wikis are used for current mechanical counts where studios do not maintain equivalent manuals. Counts are therefore version-sensitive.
- Community forum/Reddit/mod examples establish that a reaction or unmet need exists, not its prevalence. I have avoided treating upvotes or anecdotes as survey data.
- No studio in this set publishes a clean dollar or person-hour cost for its stat/legibility work. Where cost is discussed, this report uses directly observable authored variants and the systems the patch itself says were rebalanced. Anything more precise would be invented.
- Megabonk’s fast-moving community documentation and the exact Soulstone Survivors catalog count are the least stable figures. They are useful order-of-magnitude comparisons, not permanent census guarantees.
