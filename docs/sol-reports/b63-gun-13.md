## Balance reasoning

Ironclad owns the sustained-fire support niche instead of borrowing rifle or heavy single-shot tuning. Five damage at a 0.11-second fire interval yields about 45.5 raw damage per second before spread, while the 72-round feed supports a nominal 7.92 seconds of uninterrupted fire. The 2.75-second reload makes committing that reserve meaningful. Its 1,150 projectile speed and 780 behavior range hold a controlled mid-lane, but 0.09 spread and 0.0016 recoil keep the weapon from becoming a precision substitute. One point of pierce rewards aligned targets without turning each round into a crowd-clearing shell. The derived tier is 4: frontline duty hardware with high uptime and strong lane pressure, balanced by low per-round damage, handling drift, and a consequential reload.

## Art generation and visual check

I generated the art through the built-in image-generation path, using the two shipped rifle sprites only as house-style references. The final image took four iterations:

1. Established the near-black cel treatment, heavy outline, and support-weapon silhouette.
2. Moved the feed detail outside the cover so it read as a rigid external stub.
3. Clarified the stub as three short, square-seated brass-linked rounds with dark ink between each link.
4. Joined the under-barrel combo unit to the weapon silhouette with a compact rail bracket.

The installed sprite faces right and reads immediately as a light machine gun: a broad top feed cover, short rigid linked feed stub, heavy barrel, carry handle, and folded muzzle bipod remain legible at game scale. Its value structure matches the references with a near-black charcoal base and restrained top-edge highlights. The finish is matched, precise, and maintained rather than battered; seams, hinge, panels, and housings retain strong internal black ink. Brass amber is the single warm accent family. The small boxy laser/light unit has a visible lens and remains silhouette-only, with no beam, glow, or light cone. The result is a chunky flat-colour cartoon prop rather than a photographic product image.
