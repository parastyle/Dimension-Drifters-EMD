## Projectile, muzzle, and VFX decisions

This row is melee and explicitly has no projectile. It also has no bore or emission point, so no muzzle definition or muzzle flash was added; the generated projectile manifest remains at 31 and the muzzle catalog remains at 158.

Its bespoke replacement is `packages/client/public/vfx/weapons/b66/whiteout-snowshoe-ram-vfx.png`, registered as the weapon's hero contact sheet with generic fallback suppressed. The painted effect is a wide collapsing dirty off-white snow-slab fan with exactly three black crevasse chunks and one thin turquoise lower edge. It does not read as a generic crescent slash.

## Art generation and visual check

Built-in ImageGen was used on a flat `#00ff00` key and the outputs were keyed to transparent PNGs. The accepted weapon prompt used the concept entry's shape-only description; the accepted VFX prompt specified one wide broken slab fan, exactly three dark crevasse chunks, a thin turquoise lower edge, broad flat cel planes, heavy black ink, and no weapon, character, aura, text, or background.

The weapon and VFX each took two iterations. The first weapon render was rejected for overly dense realistic texture; the second used flatter cel planes and larger ink-separated shapes. The first VFX render was rejected for fine fracture texture; the second reduced the effect to three bold chunks.

I inspected the shipped transparent assets beside `x2-brimstone-gallows-rifle/part-1.png` and `x2-gravedog-auto-rifle/part-1.png`. The final weapon faces right, reads immediately as a long melee paddle-club with grip left and thick blunt rim right, and retains the open flattened-oval lattice silhouette. It has a near-black base, pale top-edge wear, crevice grime, heavy outline and internal ink, snow packed in exactly three cells, and one turquoise repair plate. Turquoise is the row-mandated single accent; no competing warm accent was introduced. The result reads as battered in-world cel art, not a product photograph or clean vector icon.
