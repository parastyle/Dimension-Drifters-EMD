## Balance reasoning

The identity is a quick-follow-up, close-range issue shotgun rather than a single-shot damage king:

- Six pellets at 4 damage give 24 raw damage per trigger pull.
- A 0.30-second semi-automatic cadence gives 80 raw burst damage per second before spread, range falloff, mitigation, and reload downtime.
- The eight-shell magazine gives it 192 raw damage per tube. Including the 1.75-second reload, sustained output is roughly 46–50 raw damage per second depending on whether the cycle is measured through the final shot or the next ready-to-fire state.
- The broad 0.28 spread and 390-unit projectile range keep that output tied to close-quarters positioning.
- A modest 820 projectile speed and 0.0024 recoil preserve weight without making follow-up shots feel sluggish.
- The 1.75-second reload is intentionally efficient for immaculate force-issued hardware, while still creating a meaningful empty-tube punish window.

## Art generation and installation

The sprite was produced with the built-in image generator, using the two shipped reference sprites as style references and the `artPrompt` above as the final production prompt. It took two image-generation iterations:

1. The first pass established the correct silhouette, furniture pattern, issue-grade finish, and accessory, but read too evenly lit and included an unwanted rear sight.
2. The second pass was a targeted edit: remove the rear sight, retain only the single bead, lower the overall value toward near-black charcoal, simplify the rendering into flatter cel planes, and strengthen internal black seams while preserving all required geometry and paint placement.

The second pass went through the existing chroma-key, component-slice, presize, install, manifest, and atlas path. The installed sprite is `packages/client/public/sprites/x2-blacktail-auto-12/part-1.png` at 256×66 pixels. The visual comparison evidence is `docs/sol-reports/b63-evidence/blacktail-auto-12.png`.

## Visual check

I rendered and inspected the installed sprite beside `x2-brimstone-gallows-rifle` and `x2-gravedog-auto-rifle`.

- Direction and mechanism: muzzle faces right; stock and grip face left. The barrel, fat under-barrel tube, slim fixed forend, receiver, pistol-grip stock, gas collar, bead sight, and loading/ejection geometry read as one mechanically plausible semi-automatic shotgun.
- Darkness: the final visible-pixel mean value is 29.3, close to the Brimstone reference at 28.4 and darker than the Gravedog reference at 43.5. Highlights are concentrated on upper edges.
- Finish: matched panels, clean fasteners, fitted shell loops, and a bright cleaned gas collar read expensive and maintained. There is no tape, improvised repair, salvage, mismatch, or neglect.
- Ink and shape: a heavy exterior contour and crisp internal lines divide the stock, receiver, ejection port, forend, tube, collar, and accessory. The tube, receiver, and stock are exaggerated enough to hold a strong game-prop silhouette.
- Colour: red tiger paint follows only the stock, pistol grip, and forend surfaces. The barrel, receiver, sight, collar, and accessory remain bare metal.
- Warm accent: amber-brass reserve-shell rims and the bead sight punctuate the dark body without competing with the red tiger furniture.
- Laser/light: a small boxy under-barrel unit has a visible lens and remains unlit, with no beam, glow, or light cone.
- Sprite hygiene: one weapon only, transparent background after keying, zero green-dominant pixels, no hands, character, text, logos, serials, shadows, dangling parts, or extra weapon.
- Style: the result reads as a dark-comic in-world prop with flat colour planes, not a product photograph.
