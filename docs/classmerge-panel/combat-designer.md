# The Dodge Roll — Combat Designer (classmerge panel)

Panel: class merge (universal kit — every character parries AND dodge rolls; user-confirmed). Role: combat design. Design only; modifies no source, runs no git.

## Premise correction, adopted from the advocate

The devil's advocate's code audit (`docs/classmerge-panel/devils-advocate.md` §0) is accepted in full: **parry is already universal** — the `"parry"` handler checks alive/budget/window/cooldown and never touches character class (`GameRoom.ts:808-829`). This panel is therefore not "unlocking parry for everyone"; it is authoring **exactly one genuinely new verb: the dodge roll.** Everything in this document is scoped to that verb. The written law it repeals must be repealed *by name*: `constants.ts:372-373` — "the parry stays the only defensive tool, so the two never overlap" — is rewritten in the same commit that ships the roll, replaced by the channel-split contract in §2 below. No orphaned law.

## Design thesis

The kit is already crowded with *answers*: parry (LMB) negates and counter-attacks everything white; jump (Space, per `docs/jumpfeel-panel/designer.md`) owns the vertical set — pits, quake rings, red low sweeps — plus the pound and the crouch distance-jump; ultimate (F) is the panic nuke. The roll earns its seat only if it is the **repositioning answer** and nothing else: the verb for *"I am standing in the wrong place and have 0.4 seconds to be somewhere else."* Parry answers attacks; jump answers the ground; roll answers **geometry** — beams, zones, lanes, rings, and your own over-commitment. The moment the roll negates an attack *better* than the verb that owns it, the kit collapses into "roll is the answer to everything" (the Souls-clone disease). Every number below is chosen to keep the roll strictly worse than parry against anything parryable, strictly worse than jump against anything jumpable, and strictly better than walking at exactly one thing: crossing space fast with a short, honest window of untouchability.

## The two-dash question — the paragraph the advocate demanded, signed

The advocate's guardrail 2: "Do not ship two dash verbs. Either dodge = crouch-dash + i-frames (one stance), or justify both in one written paragraph the panel signs." Here is that paragraph. The crouch distance-jump and the roll look like siblings on a spreadsheet (both are horizontal bursts) and are opposites in the only dimension a defensive verb lives in: **latency profile.** The crouch-leap pays its cost *up front* — 0.15 s hold detection + 0.50 s committed root before a single pixel of displacement; its own designer states that getting tagged mid-crouch cancels the leap (`docs/jumpfeel-panel/designer.md` §2.2). It is structurally incapable of answering a threat you've just read: any beam sweep, gold return, or slam you could "crouch-dodge" has resolved before you leave the ground. It is a **planned verb** — a route tool for terrain you can see coming (pits, 372 px, airborne). The roll pays its cost *afterward* — zero-latency keydown departure, then a vulnerable tail and a 3.0 s bill. It is a **reactive verb** — 188 px, ground-slaved, i-framed, useless against pits (§1.3). They share no threat row in the §2 matrix: everything the leap answers (gaps > 2 tiles, pre-planned repositioning at range) the roll cannot touch, and everything the roll answers (late reads on contact-tested attacks) the leap arrives 0.65 s too late for. **The collapse rule, conceded to the advocate:** these two verbs are distinguished by exactly two properties — latency profile and i-frames. If the panel ever cuts the i-frames (the advocate's "no i-frames at all" alternative), the roll degenerates to a short crouch-dash-without-the-crouch, the distinction thins to taste, and the honest move is to merge into one stance and ship nothing new. I-frames are not a garnish on this design; they are its admission ticket. One consolidation I *do* endorse for the panel to offer the jump wave: move the distance-leap's input from hold-Space to **hold-Shift** (tap-Shift = roll, hold ≥ 0.15 s = crouch-leap), making Shift the "dash family" key and returning Space to the clean tap-jump/air-pound pair. This resolves the advocate's Space triple-booking (§1.1 of their doc) without changing either verb's mechanics — an input-layer merge instead of a mechanics merge. The jump panel owns that call; the roll works either way.

House laws inherited and obeyed: server tick 50 ms — every duration is a multiple of 0.05 s (`docs/enemycombo-panel/designer.md` fairness floor); parry timing/latency laws from `docs/parry-panel/combat-designer.md` (§ "Parry truth", latency budget table); jump-panel netcode law — any movement verb must be a deterministic function of the command stream, replayable in prediction (`docs/jumpfeel-panel/designer.md` §4.6); the telegraph color law — white = parry, red = feet (`docs/telegraph-panel/combat-designer.md`).

## 1. THE ROLL

### 1.1 Input: **Shift, tap, fires on keydown.**

The real remaining bindings (from `docs/ultimate-panel/tech-client.md` §1's audited table, `ArenaScene.ts:1674-1676`, `:2972-3104`): LMB parry, RMB fire/beam-hold, Space = jump family (tap / hold≥0.15 s crouch-leap / mid-air pound), R grab/salvage, E interact, Q cycle, 1/2/3 slots, Tab bag, F ultimate-or-shop, T/B/C/M dev-ish. Genuinely free near WASD: **Shift**, G, X, V, Z, and the two "grammar" options — double-tap direction and a third Space meaning. The advocate's own input audit reaches the same conclusion independently: "SHIFT is the only ergonomic survivor" (`docs/classmerge-panel/devils-advocate.md` §1.1).

**Why Shift wins:**
- It is the genre-standard dash/dodge key (Hades, V Rising, every top-down action KB layout); zero teaching cost.
- It is pressed by the pinky *without leaving WASD* — the roll direction and the roll trigger are the same hand, which matters because the roll's heading IS the held movement direction (§1.3). G/X/V/Z all cost a finger off a movement key at the exact moment movement is the point.
- **The Sticky-Keys objection is answered, not ignored.** The ultimate panel rejected Shift because five rapid presses of a *mostly-not-ready* button trips the Windows Sticky Keys dialog in-browser (`docs/ultimate-panel/tech-client.md` §1). The roll is the opposite availability profile: mostly ready (3.0 s cooldown, §1.5), and its dry-press gives immediate local feedback (dust scuff + dull click, no server send — the ult panel's own `ult:dry` pattern). Windows counts *consecutive* Shift presses; kiting play re-presses WASD constantly, breaking the streak. Residual risk: a player mashing Shift while standing still with keys held. Mitigation: after 3 dry presses within 2 s, show a one-time "roll recharging" toast and swallow further local feedback — the mash stops because the game visibly heard you. If playtest still trips the dialog, the fallback is V (nearest conventionless key), not a grammar change.

**Why NOT double-tap direction:** double-tap triggers on the second tap (no added latency), but it fails on two fronts that matter more. (a) **You cannot roll in the direction you are already holding** without release→tap→tap — in a bullet-heaven you are *always* holding a direction; the most common roll (continue my heading, through the beam) becomes the most awkward input in the game. (b) **Accidental cost is catastrophic:** kiting is constant micro-re-pressing of WASD; false-positive rolls burn a 3 s defensive cooldown and a self-root tail (§1.4). A dodge with i-frames is precisely the verb that can least afford accidental triggers. Double-tap is fine for a free sprint; it is wrong for a budgeted defensive.

**Why NOT Space:** the jump panel's 150 ms tap/hold discrimination already spends Space's ambiguity budget (`docs/jumpfeel-panel/designer.md` §2.1), and mid-air Space is the pound. A third grounded meaning would need a second discrimination window, and *any* discrimination window delays the verb behind it. The roll is — after parry — the most latency-critical press in the game: the parry panel's own budget allocates just 90 ms for deliberate player response inside a 280 ms lead (`docs/parry-panel/combat-designer.md` latency table). A dodge that waits 150 ms to learn what it is has spent its entire reaction margin at the keyboard. Collision with the Space grammar is disqualifying on latency alone, before we even reach "tap-jump when I meant roll = dead in a quake ring."

**Transport: the roll rides the seq'd input command stream as an edge bit, NOT an action message — concretely, as stance 4 on the jump wave's stance machine.** This is the opposite of the ultimate's choice and for the opposite reason: the ultimate displaces nothing client-side, so it ships as a budgeted message (`docs/ultimate-panel/tech-client.md` §1); the roll is *pure displacement*, and displacement must be client-predicted or it feels like 100 ms of rubber. Jump already proves the pattern — `PredCmd.jump`, `stepPredictedVertical` replay (`prediction.ts:36-39, 100-124`). The advocate's §7 sequencing analysis is adopted wholesale: the jump wave ships the stance machine (normal/crouch/dash/pound, `stanceSeq`, schema 20); the roll is **stance 4 on that machine**, landing in wave 21b, gated on 20 being stable — building the same machinery twice is forbidden. `stepRoll(t)` is a shared pure function run identically in `GameRoom` and prediction replay; a server-side cancel (pit fall mid-roll, death) bumps `stanceSeq` and the predictor adopts — no new reconciliation concept. A side benefit is honesty: because the i-frame window and the displacement derive from the *same consumed command tick*, the invulnerability the player sees and the invulnerability the server grants are the same timeline by construction — the roll cannot desync its own promise.

**The lag-compensation number, written down (advocate §7.4 demanded it):** i-frames are granted for server ticks `[consume, consume+5)` — the tick that consumes the flagged command, no backdating, no receipt-side rewind. At 100 ms RTT the consume tick trails the physical keypress by ~2 ticks; the client renders the roll (and the i-frame tell) from the *predicted* consume tick, so the felt window and the granted window line up after reconciliation. This is deliberately the **movement-verb standard, not the parry standard**: the parry, as a message verb, compensates its delivery delay with a generous 0.52 s window; the roll compensates with prediction — press-to-departure latency is identical to the jump's press-to-liftoff, which players already accept as instant. A hit that resolves server-side in the pre-consume gap hits your pre-roll position exactly as it would have hit a walking player — the roll is never *worse* than walking under lag, and its promise (displacement + window start on the same tick) is internally consistent at any RTT.

### 1.2 Numbers on the tick grid

| Constant (proposed) | Value | Anchor |
|---|---:|---|
| Duration | **0.40 s** (8 ticks) | shorter than every full melee sentence; equals the fastest wind-up + gap |
| Distance | **188 px** (2.35 tiles) | exits any melee wedge (ranges 138–150 px) from center with margin |
| Speed schedule (px/s per tick) | **680, 640, 580, 500, 420, 340, 300, 300** | front-loaded tumble; peak 34 px/tick, under the `IMPULSE_MAX 780` ≈ 39 px/tick interp-snap envelope (`constants.ts:92-96`) |
| i-frames | **0.25 s = 5 ticks**, ticks 0–4 — the **first 62.5%** of the roll | begins on the accepted command tick, parry precedent: no startup vulnerability (`PARRY_IFRAMES` starts on acceptance) |
| Vulnerable tail | **0.15 s** (ticks 5–7, last 37.5%) — still moving 340→300 px/s | the roll's "recovery" is baked in: you are exposed while decelerating |
| Cooldown | **3.0 s**, 1 charge (economics owned by the designer sibling — see floors below) | |
| Heading | held WASD at the accept tick → else last non-zero move heading → else aim | **locked, zero mid-roll steering** — full commitment, matching the game's Lock grammar (contrast the distance jump's 45°/s soft-lock: that flight is 0.60 s and needs fudge; 0.40 s does not) |

Average speed 470 px/s = 1.47× `MOVE_SPEED 320`. Full-cycle arithmetic (the jump panel's anti-sprint test): 188 px per 3.0 s cooldown adds ~63 px/s of average mobility — the roll is a burst, never a gait.

**Why 0.25 s of i-frames and not parry-sized:** `PARRY_IFRAMES = 0.52 s`. The roll's window is **less than half the parry's**, deliberately. Parry: long window, stationary, offensive payoff, answers only white. Roll: short window, mobile, zero payoff, answers anything you can physically leave. If the roll's window approached the parry's, the parry's timing discipline would be pointless — why learn the glint when the roll forgives more and moves you too? 0.25 s is also exactly the enemy-combo panel's minimum inter-impact gap (`docs/enemycombo-panel/designer.md` fairness floor): **one roll can i-frame at most ONE beat of any authored melee sentence** — the next beat in the string always lands after the window closes. That single arithmetic fact is the roll/parry balance in miniature: roll dodges a hit, parry answers a *combo*.

**The 20 Hz honesty law:** the server grants invulnerability for ticks `[rollTick, rollTick+5)` — a whole number of ticks, no fractional edges. The client renders the i-frame tell (§3) from the same `rollTick`, so the visual window and the checked window are identical modulo the one-tick sample age every synced field already carries. What the server checks per hit: melee sector test, projectile overlap, beam contact — if `rollInvuln` ticks remain, the hit is a **null whiff** (see §2 rule 1 for why this must NOT be the `invuln` field).

### 1.3 Collision during the roll

- **Through enemies: NOT in v1 — conceded to the advocate (their §7.3 / guardrail 7).** The shared collision solver separates overlapping circles symmetrically (`collision.ts:39-45`); a pass-through exemption must be flagged per-entity and mirrored byte-exact in prediction, or the roll nondeterministically shoves a knockback ecosystem tuned around the 154 px parry truth. That is a second feature with its own bill. **V1 rule: the roll respects body collision** — rolling into a pack slides and shoves along the normal solver, exactly like sprinting into it, while the 5 i-frame ticks negate contact damage and every contact-tested hit. The roll's fantasy survives intact because its job is *leaving* crowds, not threading them. Pass-through (all 8 ticks, damage negation still i-frame-only) is priced separately as a v2 upgrade — potentially the augment lane's marquee roll augment, which gives the sibling designer a real prize to gate.
- **Through walls: never.** Roll movement runs the standard move-collision slide; a wall turns the roll into a scrape along it. No clipping, no wall-bounce tech.
- **Over pits: NO — locked.** The roll is ground-slaved (`height` stays 0). Roll across a pit tile and the existing fall rule fires mid-roll (15% chip + snapback + 0.6 s grace, `constants.ts:395-400`), cancelling the roll. Three reasons, in order: (1) verb sovereignty — the jump panel just built an entire crouch-committed distance jump whose *job* is pit crossing at the price of a 0.50 s root; a free instant pit-hop on a 3 s cooldown guts it; (2) the shadow/grounded language — quake and pit vulnerability are read from "is this cutout on the ground," and a roll is visibly on the ground; (3) texture — pit lips become genuinely dangerous terrain for panic rolls, which is exactly the kind of positional consequence a repositioning verb should respect. A 1-tile lip is a jump's job. Tap Space; it costs nothing.
- **Grounded-only trigger: `height ≤ GROUND_EPSILON`.** No air roll, ever. This single gate carries half the anti-redundancy contract (§2).

### 1.4 Cancels — what enters and exits the roll

| Transition | Ruling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Attack → roll** | **Yes, freely** — roll cancels melee recovery/follow-through any tick after the swing's resolve | This is the roll's biggest gift to the kit: melee commitment becomes survivable. You may buy your way out of a whiffed heavy swing — for a 3 s cooldown |
| **Roll → attack** | From **tick 6** (t = 0.30): attack input cancels the remaining decay into the swing | The "roll-slash" feel beat — but never from inside i-frames: an invulnerable gap-closer-into-attack is an offensive verb, and the roll is forbidden offense (§2) |
| **Roll → parry** | **Denied for all 8 ticks + 2 more** (earliest parry accept = rollTick + 10) | Anti-stacking. Roll i-frames end tick 5; earliest parry i-frames begin tick 10 → a guaranteed **0.25 s vulnerable seam** between the two windows. Roll-then-parry is a legal *sentence*, never a seamless shield |
| **Parry → roll** | **Denied while parry i-frames run** (0.52 s) | A parry already IS invulnerability; letting it move 188 px is a free invulnerable reposition — the exact "better parry" this doc exists to prevent. Roll unlocks when the parry window closes |
| **Roll → jump / jump → roll** | Both denied (roll is grounded-only; Space during a roll buffers a landing-style hop for roll-end, reusing the existing `jumpBuffer` semantics) | Vertical and horizontal escapes never compound inside one commitment |
| **Roll ↔ crouch-leap / pound** | Shift during the 0.50 s crouch-commit: denied (committed). Roll during pound recovery (0.25 s self-root): denied | Every self-root in the game stays a real root |
| **Fire (RMB) during roll** | Denied ticks 0–5 (the cutout is edge-on mid-tumble — fiction agrees), allowed ticks 6–7; a held beam channel **breaks** on roll accept | Rolling briefly silences your gun; it never holsters it |

### 1.5 Economics (floors for the designer sibling, who owns the knobs)

Proposed baseline: **1 charge, 3.0 s cooldown**, cooldown starts on roll *end*. The sibling owns charges/CDR/augment interactions (`packages/shared/src/augments.ts` economy), inside three hard floors that are combat-design law, not tuning: (1) effective cooldown never below **1.5 s** — below that, roll-chaining outruns every authored dash-lunge (the bait return covers 300 px in 0.30 s; two rolls inside its 1.15 s telegraph+dash would make the lane unlandable); (2) never more than **2 charges** — a third charge converts the roll from a decision into a gait; (3) **i-frame duration (0.25 s) and the vulnerable tail are not economic knobs.** No augment may extend the window; augments may touch distance (cap +20%), cooldown (floor above), and cosmetics.

## 2. THE ANTI-REDUNDANCY CONTRACT

### 2.0 Compliance with the advocate's admissibility rules — item by item

The advocate priced their withdrawal of the redundancy objection at four rules (`docs/classmerge-panel/devils-advocate.md` §2.5). This design accepts all four, exceeds two, and files one named refinement and one factual correction:

| Advocate rule | This design | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| No parry-reward ladder on dodge (no heal, riposte, deflect, launch) | Rules 1–2 below: roll-through is a **null whiff** via a separate `rollInvuln` field — no `parriedSeq`, no knockback, no chain, no stagger, zero offensive payoff of any kind | **Compliant, and stricter:** the enemy's combo also continues uninterrupted |
| No i-frames vs AoE/DoT ground effects (RED's area subclass) | Rule 3 below, restated as a *test-type* law: i-frames negate **contact tests** (melee sector, projectile overlap, beam line), never **position tests** (AoE resolutions, zone ticks, quake grounded checks, slam rings, pit falls) | **Compliant, with one argued refinement:** beams are classified as contact-tested — see below |
| Unusable while juggled (DI/air-parry stays the escape) | Rule 4 below: grounded-only by gate (`height ≤ GROUND_EPSILON`) — the roll does not exist in the air at all, which also protects the pound and DI | **Compliant, and stricter:** not merely "while juggled" but any airborne state |
| Cooldown ≥ 2× `PARRY_COOLDOWN` (0.6 s → floor 1.2 s) | Baseline 3.0 s = **5×**; the hard economic floor handed to the sibling designer is 1.5 s = 2.5× | **Compliant with margin** |

**The named refinement — beams sit in the contact class.** The advocate's rule grants i-frames to "projectiles and melee sector tests"; a beam is a continuous projectile — a swept contact *line*, not a ground-resolved area. Classifying it as contact-tested (i-frameable) is what gives the roll its one marquee row (§ matrix below): the threat that parry structurally cannot answer and jump cannot clear. If beams were classed as position-tested, the roll would answer *nothing* first, and the advocate's own "no i-frames at all / merge with the crouch-dash" alternative would be the honest ship — this refinement is load-bearing for the verb's existence, which is exactly why it is argued here rather than smuggled. Note the channel law survives intact in spirit: WHITE tells remain parry's (roll merely survives them at a loss), and RED *area* tells remain position's; RED *contact* tells (sweeps, rails, beams) become roll-answerable, with the economic ordering in the matrix keeping jump the better answer wherever a jump sentence exists (e.g. H1's jump-sweep-then-parry-overhead stays optimal because the roll exits the duel).

**The factual correction — the bait is not trivialized, by the combo spec's own branch logic.** The advocate's §2.1 scenario ("dodge on the feint, invincible through the return") cannot occur: the return branch fires **only on the parry event** — an unparried bait step "simply lands as a normal hit and the combo continues its normal script" (`docs/enemycombo-panel/designer.md` §3). Rolling the bait therefore never summons the return; it whiffs one white beat, pays a 3.0 s cooldown, earns nothing, and leaves the player mid-sentence. And no single roll can cover feint *and* return regardless: the return impacts ≥ 1.15 s after the bait (0.85 s wind-up + 0.30 s dash) — 4.6× the i-frame window. The bait read survives with its reward asymmetry fully intact; what the roll adds is an executable version of the reposition branch that panel already priced as the lesser answer. Their §2.5 rules, which this design satisfies, were the correct fix; their §2.1 verdict overstated the patient's condition.

**Mechanical rule 1 — the roll must not touch `invuln`.** The parry-truth section is explicit: the server counts a melee hit as *parried* when the player's center is in the sector and `invuln > 0` (`docs/parry-panel/combat-designer.md` § Parry truth; `GameRoom.ts:3479-3485`). If the roll wrote the same field, every roll through a white wedge would fire the full parry stack — `parriedSeq`, 154 px attacker knockback, chain heal, riposte stagger — making roll a *better* parry (timing window starts on YOUR schedule, not the glint's). The roll grants a **separate `rollInvuln` window whose result is a null whiff**: no knockback, no chain, no heal, no stagger, no `parriedSeq`, and — critically — **the enemy's combo continues uninterrupted.** Roll through beat 2 of the Sanren and beat 3 is still coming, at you, from an enemy you did not displace.

**Mechanical rule 2 — zero offensive payoff.** No damage, no reflect, no on-roll buff, no roll-through backstab bonus, ever. The roll's entire reward is *where you now stand*.

**Mechanical rule 3 — ground-coupled resolutions ignore roll i-frames.** Quake rings, boss slam impact zones, pool/zone damage ticks, and pit falls check position and groundedness only. Fiction: a tumble does not lift your body off the shockwave — the ground does not care that you're rolling. Mechanics: this is the wall that protects jump's identity (quakes) and positioning's identity (zones). The roll may *carry you out of* these areas — that is its job — but never *through* their damage.

**Mechanical rule 4 — grounded-only (restated as contract):** an airborne player cannot roll. The juggle escape ladder authored and priced by the enemy-combo panel — **air-parry (mastery: knocks the juggler 154 px, ends the string, pays `PARRY_LAUNCH`) > pound (panic: grounds you, eats a 0.25 s no-parry bill) > DI (walks you out of the next wedge)** — is untouched because the roll simply does not exist in the air. **Rolling during a juggle: impossible, by gate, not by tuning.** The roll re-enters the vocabulary only after your feet do.

### The threat matrix — best answer vs. merely survives

| Threat | BEST answer | Roll's standing | Why the ordering holds |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Melee telegraph cone (white)** | **Parry** — negation + 154 px knockback + chain economy + riposte | Merely survives: null whiff, one beat only (0.25 s window < 0.25 s minimum beat gap — the next beat always lands post-window), you end 188 px from your own melee range | Parry gets paid; roll pays |
| **Parry-bait gold return** | **Parry again** — chain ≥ 3 → +96 px shove + 1.0 s stagger, the mastery payout (`docs/enemycombo-panel/designer.md` §3) | Roll IS the priced lesser answer: it executes the ">90° off the lane" reposition that panel already authored — whiff → 1.50 s recover, but **no chain, no heal, no stagger**. Note the geometry: the return covers 300 px; a straight-back roll (188 px from ~264 px out) does NOT clear the envelope — the roll must be *angled*. The lane stays a read, not a reflex | The reward asymmetry was pre-priced; roll just makes the cheap branch executable under pressure |
| **Juggle launcher (white, grounded phase)** | **Parry at the door** (refuses the whole string) | Legal lesser answer pre-launch: roll leaves the wedge, no payoff. Mid-juggle: **gated out entirely** (rule 4) | Air-parry remains the only airborne counter |
| **Beam sweep** | **ROLL — this is the marquee.** Parry cannot answer beams; jump doesn't clear them (2D contact tests hit airborne players). A roll crossing the sweep line i-frames the ≤ 0.25 s of contact AND relocates you | — | The one threat class where roll is unambiguously first — the verb's reason to exist |
| **Quake ring** | **Jump** (airborne when it resolves) or **parry the ring** (the Colossus triangle, `docs/jumpfeel-panel/designer.md` §4.4) | Merely survives, and only spatially: roll i-frames do NOT apply (rule 3); an early enough roll can exit `QUAKE_REACH 260` before resolve — that's positioning, not negation | Jump's flagship stays jump's |
| **Pool / zone denial** | Don't stand in it; the roll is the **fastest legal crossing** (188 px through a pool ≈ 1 damage tick of exposure vs 2–3 walking) | i-frames do NOT negate zone ticks (rule 3) — speed is the answer, not immunity | Zones keep denying; roll discounts the toll |
| **Boss slam (impact AoE)** | **Leave the ring** — and roll is the best *late-read* exit: 188 px in 0.40 s vs 128 px walking | No i-frame on the resolution (rule 3): rolled-but-still-inside = full hit | Slams stay area tests, never timing tests |
| **Pit / gap** | Tap-jump (≤ 2 tiles) or crouch distance-jump (4 tiles) | Roll FAILS into pits by design (§1.3) — it is the one verb that makes pits *more* dangerous | The crouch-leap's monopoly survives |

The defensible one-line version: **parry answers attacks, jump answers the ground, roll answers geometry — and the roll is never allowed to answer a threat that already has an owner, only to survive it at a loss.**

## 3. THE FEEL — a paper cutout cannot tumble; it turns through the sheet

The style-bible law (`docs/paper-panel/art-director.md` § "turn through the sheet"): a cutout rotates via `scale = base × cos(π·q)` — full-face at +1, **edge-on at 0, reverse face at −1** — and all handoffs happen inside the edge-on sliver. The roll is that law made into a verb: not a fleshy shoulder-tumble but a **card cartwheeling through its own plane**.

**Animation beats (8 ticks, client-rendered from `rollTick`):**

1. **Crease pop (0–0.05 s):** the rig snaps into a diagonal fold — y-crush 0.62, skew toward the heading, weapon tucked flat against the body (foreshortened cardstock). One frame of anticipation; the dust *inhales* (the leap-Offer mirror).
2. **The turn (0.05–0.25 s, the i-frame body):** the cutout cartwheels along the roll axis through TWO full edge-on crossings — and between them shows the **reverse face: the unprinted back of the page.** Pale, desaturated, ink-free (the existing reverse-face render at scale −1). Dust wake motes (4, cheap) trail the ground line. Two **afterimage cards** — flat, single-tint copies at 60 ms spacing, fading in 120 ms — mark the path just crossed.
3. **Unfold plant (0.25–0.40 s):** the card snaps open with Back.easeOut overshoot (1.08 → 1.0), feet catch, **ink floods back in** as the print face returns. `JIGGLE_LAND_HAND_KICK` ×1.4 fires on the ownership-envelope handoff (`SpriteRig.ts:214` grammar, jump-panel tier-2 precedent) — hands, hair, and weapon overshoot and settle procedurally. T1/T2 dust scuff from the existing thump system, no camera (a 20×/run verb must not fatigue the camera — the jump panel's rule).

**The i-frame readability tell — "you can't hit the back of a page":** invulnerability is rendered as the reverse-face state — pale, unprinted, plus afterimages — for **exactly ticks 0–4**, and the ink returning at tick 5 IS the vulnerability tell. This is honest at 20 Hz because both the tell and the server check derive from the same synced `rollTick` (§1.2); the client never invents a longer or shorter window than the one being checked. Allies read it the same way (whose roll is spent, who is exposed mid-tail), and the fiction does the accessibility work: the tell is a *luminance and texture* change (pale/unprinted vs inked), not a hue, surviving grayscale per the parry panel's colorblind contract. Enemies "reading" it means: attacks that whiff through a pale card produce **no hit spark and no parry clang** — a small dry "page flutter" tick instead, so the null result is audible as null (nobody mistakes a roll-through for a parry, in either direction).

**Weight:** takeoff is light (paper flick "fwip", quieter than the plant — sound hierarchy = weight hierarchy), the plant is the accented beat: unfold overshoot + jiggle + scuff. The roll should *feel* like the cheap verb it is: a flick and a settle, no bass, no shards. Bass belongs to the pound.

## 4. CO-OP AND THE UNIVERSALITY CONSEQUENCES

**Everyone can roll — the enemy design floor rises.** Once every character owns "188 px in 0.40 s with 0.25 s of direct-hit immunity, every ~3.0 s," enemy and boss designers may — and should — author patterns that *assume* it:

1. **Beam fences** (the mandatory-roll archetype): parallel or converging sweep lines whose safe gap closes faster than 320 px/s walking but is always reachable by one roll. Parry can't answer, jump can't answer — this pattern is *the* roll exam, and it becomes legal to ship only because the kit is universal.
2. **Offset double rings:** slam/ring pairs whose shared safe band sits 150–190 px from the overlap center — walkable only with a perfect early read, rollable on a late one. Difficulty knob = band distance, not timing compression.
3. **Gold lane returns** stay as authored — the roll is the intended execution of the reposition branch (§2), which the enemy-combo panel priced but which was, honestly, hard to execute at `MOVE_SPEED`. Universality makes their cheap branch real; the reward asymmetry keeps their expensive branch desirable.
4. **The anti-roll lever is cadence, not speed:** two hits ≥ 0.30 s apart can never both be i-framed by one roll (window 0.25 s, floor gap 0.25 s). Multi-hit sentences beat roll-reflex; parry chains beat multi-hit sentences. No enemy ever needs "roll-catch" tracking or delayed-hitbox trickery — the tick math does the policing. Wind-ups still never compress (the telegraph law); density and sequencing carry difficulty.

**Shipped-boss audit** (roster: `packages/shared/src/bosses.ts` — classic, verkaln, choirmath, corvane, nul-sightline, metronome, grull, quickdraw-vane, kaido, nihil, blade-twins, dimensional-colossus, world-titan, seam-eater):
- **dimensional-colossus / world-titan (quake language):** safe — quake rings are ground-coupled (rule 3); the jump/parry/pay triangle is untouched. The roll adds only a spatial exit, which the ring radius already prices.
- **seam-eater (worm — StitchReap / RibQuake):** RibQuake safe (ground-coupled). StitchReap-class sweeps become roll-answerable — *intended*: sweeps are geometry, geometry is roll's domain.
- **kaido / blade-twins / melee-combo bosses:** safe — parry remains the paying answer to every white beat; a roll spends 3.0 s of cooldown to whiff one beat of a multi-beat sentence and interrupts nothing.
- **The one real break-risk: quickdraw-vane** (and any future instant-rail duelist) — if its identity is "sidestep the rail on the read," a 0.25 s on-demand i-frame converts a positioning test into a timing freebie. Flag to its owner: author rails as **multi-tick contact (≥ 0.30 s of live line)** or as ground-scored lanes (rule 3 coupling) so a single roll can enter but not blanket them. This is a one-line retune, not a redesign.
- **General audit rule for all future content:** any pattern whose counterplay budget was computed against max displacement `MOVE_SPEED 320` must be re-checked against a 188 px/0.40 s burst on a 3.0 s budget — chase enclosures, closing walls, and arena-denial timers are the category to sweep.

**Co-op interactions:**
- **The bodyguard rule:** the bait-return lane damages the "first body in the lane" (`docs/enemycombo-panel/designer.md` §5). An **i-framed rolling body is transparent to lane and sector checks** — the hit passes through to the next occupant. Tanking for your parrier requires *standing* in the lane; you cannot eat a hit for an ally while untouchable. Sacrifice keeps its price.
- **Legibility:** the reverse-face/afterimage tell doubles as team information — "her roll is spent, cover the tail." The pale card is visible at arena distance because it is a full-body texture state, not a particle.
- **No stacking exploits:** rolls displace only their owner and grant nothing to allies; two players rolling into the same pack each run the normal collision solver (v1 has no pass-through, §1.3) — there is no interaction surface to exploit. The bodyguard transparency above is a *damage-test* rule, independent of body collision: a rolling ally still physically occupies the lane, but an i-framed body cannot consume the hit.
- **Revive/downed:** rolling is denied while downed or channeling a revive (both are roots); a rescuer may roll *to* the downed ally but the revive channel starts only after the roll fully ends (tick 8) — no invulnerable-revive tech.
