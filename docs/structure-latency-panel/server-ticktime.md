# Server tick-time audit (reviewer 5/5)

Scope: `packages/server/src/rooms/GameRoom.ts` and `BossController.ts`, with supporting citations to `SpatialGrid.ts` and the shared schema/helpers where they explain hidden work. Estimates below are static estimates for one logical 50 ms simulation step at the stated worst case: 4 players, 80 effective enemy bodies, 120 hostile projectiles plus plausible friendly fire, 4 simultaneous beams, 12 worm slots, 48 XP Echoes, and a 28-POI arena. They are ranking bands, not measurements, and are not additive because several rows overlap. The first probes below should replace them with p95/p99 data.

## Verdict

The ordinary step should remain below 50 ms on a healthy Node process, but the path is not zero-allocation and has avoidable tail-latency multipliers. The largest issue is that immutable POI collision geometry is regenerated and linearly scanned for every projectile, player/enemy collision, and every beam sweep sample. Enemy separation is second: it performs two passes of per-enemy grid queries, sorts every query result, sorts ordinary results again, and calls `Math.hypot` before proving overlap. A delayed interval can then run three complete logical steps in one event-loop callback, multiplying either cost before the single patch broadcast.

The newest systems are mixed. Receipts and the worm runtime are good steady-state allocation designs; the tough-combo machine is mostly allocation-free between state transitions. Beams preallocate sample arrays but still allocate thousands of temporary point objects and repeatedly materialize POI-circle arrays at maximum sweep/sample counts.

## Exact step order and cost structure

`setSimulationInterval` invokes `update` every 50 ms and disables Colyseus's independent patch timer (`GameRoom.ts:1313-1318`). `update` accumulates real elapsed time, executes up to three exact 50 ms `stepSim` calls, then calls `broadcastPatch()` once for the whole batch (`GameRoom.ts:2267-2288`). Thus “per tick” below means per `stepSim`; patch encode/send is per interval callback, not per catch-up substep.

## SpatialGrid quality

The grid has the right ownership model—reused output arrays, retained cell buckets, one center cell per item, mutation updates, and conservative query padding (`SpatialGrid.ts:1-3`, `SpatialGrid.ts:34-49`, `SpatialGrid.ts:59-87`). Melee, friendly projectiles, detonations, and player beams use it. Worm segments have a separate fixed-handle grid.

The implementation still adds work to every query. `queryAabb` spreads each cell array into the output and sorts all results by insertion order (`SpatialGrid.ts:68-78`); collision then sorts ordinary candidates a second time (`GameRoom.ts:1989-2000`). `removeFromCell` is `indexOf` + `splice`, O(cell occupancy), when an item crosses cells (`SpatialGrid.ts:118-121`). In a dense cap-sized clump, the broad phase returns nearly all enemies to nearly every enemy, so the two separation passes remain O(n²).

Query radii are conservative but loose. Ordinary separation queries a full 128 px radius even though oversized bodies are separately appended (`GameRoom.ts:1980-1995`); use `ra + maxOrdinaryRadius`. Beam swept-AABB padding is correct but a long/rotating beam can cover most of the arena (`GameRoom.ts:3608-3615`). Projectile padding is correct for center-point overlap (`GameRoom.ts:6879-6890`). Worm queries conservatively add the 52 px ceiling.

Full enemy scans that bypass the grid remain in contact damage (`GameRoom.ts:2825-2860`), parry knockback (`GameRoom.ts:5511-5523`), Brand (`GameRoom.ts:5580-5589`), Emberguard (`GameRoom.ts:5452-5468`), chain-lightning seed and candidate construction (`GameRoom.ts:2981-3048`), and reflected-projectile target selection (`GameRoom.ts:7012-7029`). The first is every step; the others are burst edges that can align across four players.

The start-of-step full rebuild is redundant with much of the mutation tracking: generic AI, combo AI, boss motion, collision, spawns, and beam kills update/remove entries. However, enemy POI resolution changes positions without updating the grid (`GameRoom.ts:2776-2798`), and several death paths depend on next-step rebuild cleanup. Make those mutation sites exact, then retain the grid across steps. Worm-grid rebuilding is more redundant: it occurs at step start, after boss stepping, and after accepted worm damage (`GameRoom.ts:1928-1963`, `GameRoom.ts:4471-4492`, `GameRoom.ts:5175-5181`).

## Schema patch cost

Colyseus collections are not cleared/repopulated every step. The two `ArraySchema`s most likely to be expensive are correctly fixed: 32 receipt rows are allocated once (`GameRoom.ts:759-768`) and the worm's 12 segment rows are allocated once (`BossController.ts:342-346`). XP Echoes also keep flight positions client-derived and mutate only launch/delivery edges, an excellent patch-cost design (`packages/shared/src/state.ts:264-281`). There is no evidence here of an `ArraySchema`/`MapSchema` operation that forces a full collection re-encode every step.

The patch cost is dirty scalar churn:

- Every projectile changes x/y each step even though vx/vy are synced specifically for client extrapolation (`GameRoom.ts:6782-6788`; `packages/shared/src/state.ts:283-304`). This is the largest avoidable moving-entity patch stream.
- Every moving enemy changes x/y. Legacy and authored melee enemies also change `windup` each windup tick (`GameRoom.ts:5775-5779`, `GameRoom.ts:5994-6033`). Enemy x/y are `number`, not `float32` (`packages/shared/src/state.ts:161-178`).
- Four players legitimately churn x/y plus six reconciliation/momentum fields (`GameRoom.ts:2387-2400`; `packages/shared/src/state.ts:90-118`). With only four players this is not the primary cost.
- A beam row republishes many fields every step, including duplicate semantics (`effectiveLength` and `length`, `width` and `halfWidth`) plus continuous origin, previous origin, angles, heat, and intensity (`GameRoom.ts:3455-3493`; `packages/shared/src/state.ts:306-329`). Schema dirty checks should suppress identical constant assignments, but active fields still churn.
- Boss telegraph `t` and active geometry are continuous 20 Hz fields (`BossController.ts:1931-1939`, `BossController.ts:1977-2003`). Tough-combo rows additionally carry `enemy.windup` even though their timing is tick-anchored server-side.
- Receipts are heap-allocation-free, but every accepted hit overwrites eleven schema fields in a ring row and computes a `Math.hypot` (`GameRoom.ts:2203-2237`). A beam pulse hitting many targets can rotate through all 32 rows in one step, making the full ring dirty.
- Worm pose publication is already throttled to every other step (10 Hz), while integrity/armor are quantized bytes (`BossController.ts:1377-1415`). Masks are assigned every step but should be clean when unchanged. This is the model to copy.
- Elapsed time is projected at 1 Hz and level-window time at 10 Hz with explicit dirty checks (`GameRoom.ts:2240-2252`). These are compliant.

## Substeps and scheduling hygiene

The accumulator is deterministic and drift-correcting: it consumes the real `deltaMs` supplied by Colyseus in exact 50 ms units (`GameRoom.ts:2267-2281`). It caps accumulated time at 175 ms, so one callback runs at most three complete steps and retains at most 25 ms. The downside is event-loop monopolization: after a stall, AI, collision, beams, projectiles, Echoes, cleanup, and schema mutation all run three times back-to-back before one patch.

Almost all gameplay work correctly needs logical-step cadence for deterministic outcomes. Work that does not need to repeat inside a catch-up batch includes stale-key pruning/copying, telemetry, patch-only projections, and potentially static-grid maintenance. Patch encode already runs once per batch. Do not lower projectile/collision cadence without swept tests; instead remove their avoidable work and, if catch-up still blocks, yield between logical steps while preserving their order.

Timer hygiene is otherwise strong. There is one simulation interval, the independent patch interval is disabled, and there are no `Date.now`, `performance.now`, `setTimeout`, extra `setInterval`, or Colyseus clock reads in either hot path. This avoids clock disagreement but also means no deadline/overrun telemetry exists. The accumulator corrects interval drift; it does not prevent a costly catch-up callback from delaying every room on the same Node thread.

## First three instrumentation probes

1. **Phase and catch-up histogram at `update`/`stepSim`.** Add monotonic timestamps around: input+grid; player movement/XP/director; player attacks+beams+melee; enemy AI+boss; projectiles+zones; collision+contact+tail; and `broadcastPatch` (`GameRoom.ts:2274-2288`, `GameRoom.ts:2292-2934`). Record p50/p95/p99/max, logical tick, substep index/count, entity counts, and accumulator backlog in preallocated histograms. This confirms whether separation, projectiles, beams, or encode owns the budget and exposes 3-step callback amplification.

2. **Collision-work and allocation/GC correlation.** Increment integer counters in `SpatialGrid.queryAabb` for cells, candidates, and sorts; in projectile/beam paths for POIs tested, compound circles materialized, beam samples, sample×candidate tests, and friendly/hostile projectiles (`SpatialGrid.ts:60-78`, `GameRoom.ts:3496-3665`, `GameRoom.ts:6780-6957`). Alongside them, use a process-level `PerformanceObserver` for GC duration and sampled `heapUsed` deltas, correlated by room/tick without per-tick object creation. This directly validates findings 1–3 and 7.

3. **Patch encode/send probe around `broadcastPatch`.** Measure encode wall time and encoded byte length, plus dirty-field counts grouped as player/enemy/projectile/beam/telegraph/worm/receipt (`GameRoom.ts:2282-2288`). Run the same synthetic room with stationary projectiles, moving projectiles, four active beams, and a receipt burst to separate entity simulation from schema cost. Confirm whether Colyseus encodes once for all four clients or performs per-client work in the deployed configuration.

The synthetic room should be deterministic and run long enough to fill worm history and reach steady GC: 4 moving/firing players, 80 mixed enemies packed first into a dense 2×2-cell clump and then spread, 120 hostile projectiles, the maximum sustainable friendly shots, four maximum-turn beams, 48 Echoes, approximately 150 zones, the split 12-slot worm, four active tough combos, and simultaneous hit/receipt bursts. Report warm p95/p99 and maximum callback time separately from per-logical-step time.

**Highest-leverage fix: precompute and spatially index immutable POI collision circles once per map, then route projectile, beam, player, and enemy collision through allocation-free queries of that cache.**
