# Dimension Drifters — Competitive Parity Report

**Research snapshot:** 2026-07-19  
**Mandate:** research and strategy only; no implementation scope is implied by this document.

## Executive read

The five games that matter most are **Soulstone Survivors** (the closest breadth/mastery benchmark), **Vampire Survivors** (the unlock-and-discovery machine), **Risk of Rain 2** (online co-op scaling and item economy), **Hades** (weapon feel, identity, and ceremony), and **Megabonk** (the current proof that spatial comedy, quests, and instantly readable clips can break out). Brotato is the strongest value/UI benchmark and The Spell Brigade is the most immediately useful co-op feature benchmark, but those five define the product bar.

The three largest gaps are:

1. **Return scaffolding:** the branch has a compelling run and metagame, but not a shipped quest/achievement ledger, daily or weekly challenge, leaderboard, or rich run-end dossier. Competitors turn the same content into hundreds of visible reasons to return.
2. **Co-op productization:** Colyseus supplies real online play, but market parity requires explicit player-count scaling, reconnect and late-join behavior, recovery roles for dead players, cooperative objectives, readable loot ownership, and team-result ceremony.
3. **Breadth outside the arsenal:** 316+ weapons is an unusually strong headline, while the currently provable dimension and regular-enemy breadth, authored music, controller experience, accessibility suite, and localization pipeline do not yet match the best launch-ready packages.

**Highest-leverage move:** build one server-authored **Rift Contract** spine that produces a fixed-seed daily/weekly dimension chain, visible quest credit, a signed result receipt, a run dossier, and shareable personal/squad standings. It reuses map RNG, depth, World Tiers, bosses, gear, pets, and the weapon bank; one system closes several of the largest parity gaps at once.

## Comparison basis

This report treats the binding [metagame decisions](../metagame-panel/DECISIONS.md) as the product direction and uses the current branch as evidence where the older [README](../../README.md) and [backlog](../../BACKLOG.md) describe an earlier scaffold. The comparison target is therefore:

- a top-down online co-op arena roguelite with manual aim plus combos, parry, dodge, slide, jump, and ultimates;
- 316+ weapons, augments, gear, pets, and one recharging Drive resource;
- five defined dimensions, a depth-chain run structure, procedural arena layouts, and approximately fourteen named boss definitions, including the worm, colossus, and Vastaghar-scale set pieces ([dimension definitions](../../packages/shared/src/dimensions.generated.ts), [boss roster](../../packages/shared/src/bosses.ts));
- a permanent identity wardrobe, permanent pets, a losable weapon bank, extraction, World Tier prestige, and the physical hat tower;
- Colyseus online co-op and a Madness-style stitched paper-cutout body.

Competitor counts below are approximate because live games, DLC, alternate forms, variants, and wiki taxonomy change the totals. Prices are U.S. Steam list prices before temporary promotions; reception is the visible Steam user-review result, rounded, as researched on 2026-07-19. A feature is not counted as parity merely because it is designed: this matrix compares shipped competitor evidence with working branch evidence or a clearly identified launch target.

### Matrix conclusion

The scorecard is intentionally severe: **1 ahead, 2 par, 10 behind**. That does not imply ten independent large systems. Most deficits cluster around three shared spines: (1) contracts/results/competition, (2) co-op lifecycle, and (3) launch usability and content proof. Solving those spines is more efficient than copying every competitor mode.

## 3. Top 10 parity moves, ranked by impact / effort

Impact and effort are relative to this branch: **H/M/L**. “Pre-launch-critical” means the foundation should be present before commercial launch; it does not require the final live-service quantity on day one.

### 1. Ship the Rift Contract spine

**Impact: H · Effort: M · PRE-LAUNCH-CRITICAL foundation; POST-LAUNCH live cadence.** Add a server-authored Contract definition containing a fixed seed, dimension/depth chain, allowed squad-size band, World Tier normalization, two or three modifiers, an optional objective, and a reward tag. The result receipt should include the starting loadout hash, bank-risk snapshot, splits, downs/revives, and completion signature. Expose one daily and one weekly Rift, 8–12 rotating evergreen trials, personal/squad bests, visible contract rewards, and a clean share card. This one data path reuses map RNG and existing run state while creating dailies, challenges, achievement hooks, run-end ceremony, creator races, and eventually verified leaderboards. If authoritative validation is not ready, ship honest **personal and friends/squad standings** first—never market a locally trusted global board as fair.

### 2. Productize the whole co-op lifecycle

**Impact: H · Effort: H · PRE-LAUNCH-CRITICAL.** Define behavior for 1–4 players at every state: use additional spawns and elite mixes before health-sponge scaling; preserve per-player reward pace; add readable pings and weapon/gear ownership; issue a reconnect token and authoritative state snapshot; constrain late joins to safe depth transitions; and make a downed/dead player useful through pet-command, ward/ghost, or rescue-objective play until revival. Add two-player interaction hooks grounded in existing verbs—parry a projectile off an ally, Drive-overload a shared device, carry a downed dummy while sliding, or combine ultimates against a boss break bar—and record rescues/setup damage in the result screen. The target is The Spell Brigade’s interaction and Temtem: Swarm’s death handling with RoR2’s economy clarity, not four isolated solo builds sharing a room.

### 3. Establish a dimension encounter-density floor

**Impact: H · Effort: H · PRE-LAUNCH-CRITICAL.** Keep the five dimensions, but require each to change decisions through at least one hazard system, one rotating objective, three meaningful points of interest or route choices, six common enemy roles, two elite behaviors, and one native boss, with cross-dimensional invaders and the existing named bosses widening the mix. A practical first bar is roughly **30 biome-owned common encounters, 10 elites, and the existing ~14 boss definitions**, assembled so no two consecutive depths repeat the same pressure package. Frostfell might alter traction and shatter cover; Ashlands could turn Drive heat into route pressure; Neon-Cyber might expose hackable turrets and line hazards. Procedural topology is the delivery mechanism, not the variety itself.

### 4. Give 316+ weapons a PSO2/Hades family identity pass

**Impact: H · Effort: M–H · PRE-LAUNCH-CRITICAL.** Classify the arsenal into roughly 10–12 named families, each with a one-sentence combat promise and a standard identity card: range, normal cadence, held/charged behavior, combo grammar, movement effect, parry relationship, Drive spend or overheat rule, ultimate role, audio transient, and silhouette language. Choose about 24 “golden” launch exemplars—two per family—for bespoke Hades-grade tuning and presentation; let the wider catalog inherit proven family code and then earn mutations. The bank UI should preserve discovery, mastery, favorite, loss, and recovery history for each named weapon. This turns “316” from a quantity claim into PSO2-like family loyalty and makes balancing tractable without deleting the joyful excess.

### 5. Make extraction, death, and prestige into a run dossier

**Impact: H · Effort: M · PRE-LAUNCH-CRITICAL.** At every run end, show depth chain, time, World Tier, seed/Contract, bosses, build timeline, damage and kills, damage prevented, parries, dodges, slides, rescues, Drive uptime/overheats, pet contribution, loot banked, and weapons permanently lost. Extraction should visibly transfer the carried weapon into the Armory; death should give each lost item a brief paper “funeral card”; prestige should stage the weapon-bank farewell before the next hat lands on the tower. Include team contribution panels that value setup, tanking, and rescue rather than only damage, then generate one uncluttered share image. DD’s real stakes are already more distinctive than a generic victory screen; ceremony makes players feel and broadcast them.

### 6. Turn the metagame into a visible 96-Contract launch ledger

**Impact: H · Effort: M · PRE-LAUNCH-CRITICAL.** Seed the same Contract schema with a finite launch mastery board—for example 24 progression goals, 24 weapon-family goals, 16 gear/set goals, 12 pet/fusion goals, 12 boss/World Tier goals, and 8 co-op goals. Every card must show prerequisite, measurable rule, and a specific identity reward: a wardrobe component, pet trait, family augment, named weapon lead, hat ornament, or new encounter—not a second currency. Chain early cards to teach bank/extract/death, parry, Drive and overheat, gear replacement, pet care, dimension routing, and prestige. Halls of Torment and Megabonk prove that a quest ledger multiplies perceived content; DD can make it feel diegetic as Rift paperwork pinned around the wardrobe and Armory.

### 7. Lock controller and combat-accessibility parity

**Impact: H · Effort: M · PRE-LAUNCH-CRITICAL.** Implement full gameplay and menu remapping, automatic glyph switching, correct focus recovery, twin-stick aim curves/deadzones, optional target friction, optional auto-fire per weapon family, hold/toggle choices, text and UI scaling, subtitle treatment, redundant color/shape telegraphs, and sliders/toggles for shake, flash, hit stop, damage-number density, particle density, and reduced motion. Add practice modifiers for enemy speed, enemy damage, and player durability while preserving ordinary unlock progress; only verified competitive Contracts need a standardized rules flag. Run every Wardrobe, Armory, pet, bank-loss, and prestige flow without a mouse. Dense co-op effects and active precision make this a reach and fairness feature, not polish.

### 8. Make every World Tier introduce a rule, not just a multiplier

**Impact: M–H · Effort: M · PRE-LAUNCH foundation; POST-LAUNCH breadth.** Package tiers in readable groups so each step adds at least one behavior: an enemy mutation, elite affix, escalated dimension hazard, alternate boss phase, tighter extraction choice, or changed bank condition, with the next packet previewed before the player accepts prestige and a possible bank wipe. Recombine a controlled vocabulary—mirrored projectiles, armor breaks, tether pairs, parasitic pickups, Drive-static zones—rather than authoring 30 unrelated modes. Tie milestones to hat layers, family mastery, and rare gear textures. The existing World Tier and farewell ritual already beat a flat difficulty dropdown emotionally; explicit rule packets give it Hades Heat/Soulstone curse replay value.

### 9. Author the five-dimension audio arc

**Impact: M · Effort: M · PRE-LAUNCH minimum; POST-LAUNCH expansion.** Build on the existing AudioBus and five ambience beds with one reactive suite per dimension: exploration, wave escalation, elite pressure, boss, extraction, and failure stems that can layer without restarting. Give each weapon family a recognizable attack transient plus shared parry, Drive-full, Drive-spend, and overheat grammar; reserve unique stings for bank loss, successful extraction, pet evolution, and prestige. Establish a mix budget that ducks low-value impacts before critical telegraphs when four players and hundreds of enemies overlap. The goal is not Hades’ track count at launch; it is that a blind clip communicates dimension, family, danger, and outcome.

### 10. Externalize first, then localize the launch surface

**Impact: M–H · Effort: M–H · PRE-LAUNCH-CRITICAL pipeline; POST-LAUNCH language expansion.** Move every UI string, Contract rule, item affix, tutorial prompt, subtitle, error, and controller glyph label into stable keys now; support plural rules, gender-safe phrasing, font fallback, variable-safe templates, and 30–40% layout expansion. Localize the store page, onboarding, settings, core HUD, Wardrobe/Armory/bank, and launch Contracts as one testable vertical slice before scaling to a market-informed initial set such as Simplified Chinese, Brazilian Portuguese, LatAm Spanish, German, French, Japanese, and Korean. Pseudolocalization and screenshot sweeps should gate merges. Externalization is the pre-launch parity requirement; additional language quantity can follow demand without reopening every scene.

## 4. Differentiation parity must not dilute

### 1. Active combat grammar inside a bullet-heaven

Manual aim plus combo, parry, dodge, slide, jump, and ultimate play is the clearest mechanical separation from almost every direct competitor. Accessibility options may automate individual burdens, but the default product must not collapse into passive orbiting weapons or undifferentiated cooldown soup. Family identity, enemy design, and co-op objectives should create more reasons to use these verbs together.

### 2. Identity with visible, comprehensible stakes

The replacement-texture wardrobe, permanent pet companion, losable banked weapons, one understandable Drive resource, and physically stacked prestige hats form one unusually coherent identity loop. Do not replace it with a generic permanent-stat tree, multiple currencies, insurance that erases death, or a prestige menu detached from the dummy’s body. The absurd visual persistence and the painful Armory farewell are the point.

### 3. Dimension-hopping paper-cutout spectacle in true online co-op

The stitched Madness-style dummy, springing hat tower, weapon slapstick, pets, dimension portals, segmented worm, colossus, and Vastaghar-scale flagship can produce a recognizable screenshot before the logo appears. Do not chase competitor content counts through homogeneous arena reskins, near-duplicate weapons, or effects density that hides the puppets. Parity work should reveal this spectacle—especially to a four-player audience—not sand off its handmade weirdness.

## Recommended product bar

For launch, the most credible parity promise is not “more of everything.” It is: **five tactically distinct dimension chains; a family-legible 316+ arsenal; 30-ish regular encounter behaviors plus 10 elites and the existing boss slate; a complete 1–4-player lifecycle; a 96-card mastery ledger; World Tier rule packets; controller/accessibility coverage; and one signed Contract/result spine.** Localization breadth, more authored music, expanded trials, and public competitive seasons can then grow on foundations that do not need to be replaced.

That package preserves the reasons Dimension Drifters is already unusual while closing the surfaces players use to judge whether an ambitious roguelite is finished.
